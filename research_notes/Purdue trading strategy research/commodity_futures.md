## Which Purdue researchers have studied futures price behavior in ways that suggest a trading rule?

### Takeaway
The strongest Purdue-origin, rule-testing work is the 1980s technical-trading-systems program of Scott Irwin (then a Purdue PhD candidate), B. Wade Brorsen (then a Purdue assistant professor) and Louis Lukac (Purdue PhD). They simulated channel, moving-average and similar trend systems on futures portfolios and found significant net returns in 1978-84. Later replication by Park and Irwin, both at Illinois and so not Purdue work, found the returns did not persist. Current Purdue work is led by Mindy Mallory (Clearing Corporation Chair, Purdue Ag Econ). It covers USDA-report announcement effects, price discovery between nearby and deferred contracts, and bid-ask microstructure, and it is descriptive rather than a tested trading rule. Center for Commercial Agriculture extension pieces describe basis and futures-spread seasonality for hedgers.

### Cited Findings
**1. Irwin and Brorsen (1985), technical trading systems in US futures** [Purdue: verified]
- The 1985 working paper / AAEA paper asks whether technical trading systems are profitable in US futures. Irwin is listed as "Ph.D. Candidate in the Department of Agricultural Economics at Purdue University" and Brorsen as "Assistant Professor" in the same department — [AgEcon Search, aaea-1985-045](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/278568/files/aaea-1985-045.pdf) (seen only through a search summary; the fetch returned 403); see also [farmdoc NCCC-134 1985 proceedings](https://farmdoc.illinois.edu/assets/meetings/nccc134/conf_1985/pdf/confp16-85.pdf) (image-only scan, could not be parsed).
- Methods named in the summary: only out-of-sample returns are analyzed, and simulated returns are tested for statistical significance — [AgEcon Search](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/278568/files/aaea-1985-045.pdf).

**2. "A test of futures market disequilibrium using twelve different technical trading systems" (Lukac, Brorsen and Irwin, 1988, Applied Economics)** [Purdue: verified for the Irwin and Brorsen era, per the source above]
- The study simulated 12 technical systems on a portfolio of 12 commodities from 1978 to 1984. Seven systems had significant gross returns, and four of the 12 had significant net (after-cost) and risk-adjusted returns — [Scilit record](https://scilit.com/publications/c283eba5ed79a2127bb9f8b70eb70fe9); [scite](https://scite.ai/reports/a-test-of-futures-market-1rOvMj).
- Companion JFM paper, vol. 8(1), 1988, pp. 1-13. My understanding is that this is Lukac, Brorsen and Irwin, "Similarity of computer guided technical trading systems", but the search results did not confirm the title — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v8y1988i1p1-13.html).
- The work was extended in Lukac and Brorsen (1990), "A comprehensive test of futures market disequilibrium", Financial Review 25(4): 593-622. That paper simulated 23 trading systems on 30 futures markets over 11 years — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/finrev/v25y1990i4p593-622.html).
- Related Purdue dissertation: Milton S. Boyd, "Disequilibrium in commodity futures markets", Purdue PhD, advisor Brorsen. It simulated five technical systems on seven commodities over 1960-1986, found significant returns, and rejected weak-form efficiency — [Purdue e-Pubs](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/dissertations/AAI9132426).
- Out-of-sample replication (not Purdue: Park and Irwin, Illinois). The earlier successful performance of the Lukac-Brorsen-Irwin systems "did not persist in the subsequent sample period". After a data-snooping correction (White's Reality Check / SPA), the best rules made significant profits in only 2 of 17 US futures contracts. JFM 30(7), 2010, pp. 633-659 — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v30y2010i7p633-659.html); [Illinois IDEALS thesis](https://www.ideals.illinois.edu/items/84261); [AgEcon Search](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/19039/files/cp05pa01.pdf).

**3. Mindy Mallory (Purdue Ag Econ)** [Purdue: verified for current affiliation; era-dependent for papers before about 2019]
- Purdue profile: Associate Professor and Clearing Corporation Charitable Foundation Endowed Chair of Food & Agricultural Marketing, Department of Agricultural Economics — [Purdue CCA team page](https://ag.purdue.edu/commercialag/home/team/mindy-mallory).
- RePEc lists her affiliation as Purdue Ag Econ. Her futures papers include: price discovery between nearby and deferred contracts in storable and non-storable markets (arXiv working paper, 2017); the components of the bid-ask spread in corn futures (Agricultural Economics 49(3), 2018); live cattle electronic bid-ask spread behavior (AAEA 2016); short-term lean hog price density forecasts (ERAE 2018); and volatility spillovers among crude oil, ethanol and corn futures (JARE 37(2), 2012). RePEc lists no paper on USDA reports, price limits or trading rules — [RePEc/EconPapers author page](https://econpapers.repec.org/RAS/pba656.htm). Most of these predate her move to Purdue (earlier series are listed under Iowa State/Illinois), so treat them as era-dependent.
- Hu and Mallory (NCCC-134, 2022) is a Purdue-era intraday event study. It measures how USDA reports affect Dalian Commodity Exchange soybean-complex futures, using intraday prices and volume from January 2010 to April 2020. The paper finds USDA reports have "substantial effects" on Chinese prices. It notes that US futures absorb USDA information "within a matter of minutes", and that the US-China soybean price link broke down after the 2018 tariffs — [AgEcon Search PDF](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/329790/files/Hu_Mallory_NCCC-134_2022.pdf) (seen only through a search summary; full text returned 403).
- Mallory and Naughton (2020) is a white paper on the CME's 2018 maximum order-size rule change in grain calendar spreads. It is cited in Peng, Hu and Robe (NCCC-134, 2021), which studies how order-size limits affect volatility and liquidity in the corn calendar spread — [Peng, Hu, Robe 2021](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/316410/files/Peng_Hu_Robe_NCCC-134_2021.pdf).
- Mallory, Garcia and Serra (NCCC-134, 2015) studies how nearby and deferred futures quotes adjust to information — [AgEcon Search](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/285834/files/Mallory_Garcia_Serra_NCCC_134_2015.pdf) (Purdue affiliation in 2015 not confirmed; probably Illinois at the time).

### Inferences
- The only Purdue work that directly tests trading rules is the 1980s Lukac/Brorsen/Irwin/Boyd program, and its published out-of-sample follow-up (non-Purdue) says the edge decayed. Purdue's modern contribution is event-study and microstructure evidence that informs event windows, not ready-made rules.
- Brorsen moved to Oklahoma State and Irwin to Ohio State and then Illinois. Their later, non-Purdue work (such as Park and Irwin) should not be credited to Purdue.

## What exact rules or event windows are used, and which parameters could be tuned?

### Takeaway
The Purdue-era technical systems were trend-following daily-bar rules (channel breakouts, moving-average crossovers and similar) with parameters re-optimized on a rolling basis and evaluated only out of sample. Purdue's USDA-report work uses intraday event windows of minutes around the release time. The extension basis and seasonality material is a monthly or seasonal calendar rule.

### Cited Findings
- Lukac et al. (1988) used 12 systems and 12 commodities over 1978-84, with significance tests on gross, net and risk-adjusted returns — [Scilit](https://scilit.com/publications/c283eba5ed79a2127bb9f8b70eb70fe9). Lukac and Brorsen (1990) used 23 systems, 30 markets and 11 years — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/finrev/v25y1990i4p593-622.html).
- The design uses out-of-sample returns only, meaning parameters are chosen on prior data and then traded forward — [AgEcon Search 1985](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/278568/files/aaea-1985-045.pdf) (summary).
- The Park and Irwin (2010) replication used the same rule families and corrected for data snooping (White's Reality Check, Hansen's SPA) — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v30y2010i7p633-659.html).
- In Hu and Mallory (2022), the event is the USDA report release (WASDE/Crop Production timing), measured in intraday bars. The cited prior literature shows US futures absorb the information within minutes — [Hu and Mallory 2022](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/329790/files/Hu_Mallory_NCCC-134_2022.pdf).
- Non-Purdue context on windows (useful for parameter ranges). Anderson, McKenzie and Noyes (NCCC-134, 2024) found significant corn and soybean reactions 30 minutes after the WASDE, with soybean reactions still growing after that — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/ags/nccc24/379003.html). Releasing the WASDE during trading hours raised volatility only briefly — [search summary of the Lehecka and related literature](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/142491/files/SAEA2013_Lehecka_Paper.pdf).
- Basis seasonality: basis tends to strengthen from October (harvest) to June — [Purdue CCA 2025](https://ag.purdue.edu/commercialag/home/resource/2025/09/crop-basis-review-2024-2025-and-early-indicators-for-2025-2026).

### Inferences
- Tunable parameters for a modern port:
  - Trend systems: channel length N, MA fast and slow lengths, stop distance, re-optimization window length, and how often to re-optimize.
  - Event studies: pre-event flat window, entry delay after release (0 to 5 minutes), holding horizon (5, 15, 30 or 60 minutes, or to the close), and surprise or direction filter.

### Gaps
- I did not find exact Lukac et al. parameter grids.
- There is no Purdue-authored surprise metric (for example, report vs. analyst survey) with numbers.

## Could the pattern be ported to ES, NQ, RTY, YM, CL, GC, SI, ZN, 6E, 6J?

### Takeaway
Yes for two families. First, the trend-following channel and MA systems port directly to any futures contract on 1-minute bars resampled to daily or intraday bars, but their documented edge decayed after the 1980s. Second, the event-window structure ports to CL around the EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report (Wednesday 10:30 ET) and to ZN, ES and the FX futures around CPI, NFP and FOMC releases. Basis and seasonal-storage patterns are ag-specific and need cash-price data, so they do not port.

### Cited Findings
- Lukac and Brorsen (1990) tested 30 futures markets, which included financials and metals as well as ags, so the trend systems were never ag-only — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/finrev/v25y1990i4p593-622.html) (the exact market list was not verified).
- Mallory's own work links crude oil, ethanol and corn futures volatility (volatility spillovers, JARE 2012), which supports cross-market transmission from CL to ag — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RAS/pba656.htm).
- Non-Purdue evidence that the event-window idea works on energy and rates: "VPIN, Jump Dynamics and Inventory Announcements in Energy Futures Markets" (JFM) and work on Treasury futures volatility around macro announcements — [vLex JFM](https://law-journals-books.vlex.com/vid/vpin-jump-dynamics-and-855619120); [IDEAS UCSD](https://ideas.repec.org/p/cdl/ucsdec/qt7rd4g3bk.html); [NY Fed SR307](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr307.pdf).

### Inferences
- **Testable with 1-minute OHLCV plus an event calendar:**
  - (a) Trend systems on daily or intraday resamples, with rolling re-optimization.
  - (b) Event-window drift or reversal after a release: CL at EIA (10:30 ET Wednesday), ZN/ES/NQ/6E/6J at CPI/NFP (8:30 ET) and FOMC (14:00 ET). Measure the first 1-5 minute move, then test continuation versus reversal over 15-60 minutes.
  - (c) Volatility expansion in the release minute, for straddle-like breakout entries.
- **Needs data beyond OHLCV plus a calendar:**
  - Surprise sign or size, which needs consensus forecasts.
  - Basis, which needs cash prices.
  - COT/hedging pressure, which needs CFTC COT data (weekly and public, so easy to add).
  - Calendar spread signals, which need deferred-contract bars.
  - Bid-ask and order-size effects, which need quote or depth data.
- Limit-move studies apply mainly to ags and livestock. For ES/NQ, the analogue is CME price-limit and circuit-breaker bands, which rarely trigger.

### Gaps
- I found no Purdue paper that tests EIA, macro-release or equity-index futures rules.

## What were the findings, and did any test trading rules directly?

### Takeaway
Only the Purdue-era Irwin/Brorsen/Lukac/Boyd studies tested rules directly. They found significant returns in 1960-1986 data (4 of 12 systems significant net of costs in 1978-84). Modern data-snooping-robust replication by Illinois authors found only 2 of 17 contracts profitable, and the original systems' edge did not persist. Purdue's event-study work (Hu and Mallory) finds large, fast report effects but tests no trading rule.

### Cited Findings
- 1978-84: 7 of 12 systems had significant gross returns, and 4 of 12 had significant net and risk-adjusted returns — [Scilit](https://scilit.com/publications/c283eba5ed79a2127bb9f8b70eb70fe9).
- Boyd's dissertation (1960-1986, 5 systems, 7 commodities) found significant technical returns and rejected weak-form efficiency — [Purdue e-Pubs](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/dissertations/AAI9132426).
- In the later sample, the earlier successful performance did not persist, and only 2 of 17 contracts were significant after the data-snooping correction (Park and Irwin, not Purdue) — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v30y2010i7p633-659.html); [Illinois IDEALS](https://www.ideals.illinois.edu/items/84261).
- USDA reports have substantial intraday effects on Dalian soybean futures in 2010-2020, and US markets absorb the news within minutes (Hu and Mallory, Purdue) — [AgEcon Search](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/329790/files/Hu_Mallory_NCCC-134_2022.pdf).
- A Purdue-hosted, NASS-authored item found soybeans rose on 15 of 24 Crop Production report days over five years, which does not support "prices always fall on report day" — [Purdue-hosted PDF](https://ag.purdue.edu/usda/gl/arms-pages/armsiii1/materials/price-reaction-2024.pdf). Caveat: the sample is small and it is not peer-reviewed.

### Inferences
- Caveats for any backtest:
  - Publication-era decay.
  - Data-snooping across many systems and parameter sets (use White's or SPA-style tests, which matches the user's existing funnel and holdout practice).
  - Speed: the information is absorbed in minutes, so any event edge on 1-minute bars must survive the first-bar slippage.

### Gaps
- I have no Sharpe ratios, profit factors or per-trade returns for the Purdue systems, because the full texts were inaccessible (403 or scanned images).
- I found no Purdue-authored study of COT positioning, roll or term-structure returns, or limit-move continuation with numbers.
