# Purdue (Daniels/Krannert) Research on Return Anomalies, Time-Series Predictability and Timing Strategies

Scope note: 19 tool calls. I read two full texts (Gulen & Woeppel JFQA convexity paper; Cooper, McConnell & Ovtchinnikov 2009 January Barometer trading paper). The other papers are covered from abstracts, faculty pages and secondary summaries, and are flagged as such.

## Which Purdue-affiliated faculty or PhD researchers have published papers that document predictable patterns in asset returns?

### Takeaway
The main Purdue group is Huseyin Gulen (Daniels finance department head) and his co-authors and students (Adem Atmaz, Fangcheng Ruan, Michael Woeppel, Stefano Cassella). The second group is John McConnell and his co-authors on calendar effects. Michael Cooper (Krannert until about 2006) adds market-state momentum and overnight returns. Alessio Saretto (Krannert in the 2000s) adds option-implied-volatility signals. The two most futures-relevant, low-obscurity-risk findings are **price-path convexity** (Gulen & Woeppel) and the **calendar effects** (turn of month, January Barometer) from McConnell.

### Inferences
- Gulen and his students form a coherent research line on extrapolative beliefs. Convexity, absolute-strength momentum, DOX (the degree of extrapolation in investors' beliefs) and contrarian/extrapolator momentum all model how investors over-weight recent price moves. That makes the line a natural source of time-series signals for a single index future.
- Cliff/Cooper/Gulen: Gulen is a current Purdue author. I did not confirm Gulen's affiliation on the paper itself, but his Purdue faculty page lists related media coverage.

## What exact rules define each strategy or signal, and which parameters could be tuned?

### Takeaway
Three signals have fully specified, simple rules that I verified from full text or the abstract: convexity, the January Barometer long/T-bill rule, and turn of the month. The others (absolute strength, market-state momentum, DOX) are known from summaries only.

### Cited Findings

**1. Price-path convexity (Gulen & Woeppel)**

Source for this section: [JFQA PDF](https://jfqa.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/25822_Price-path-convexity.pdf).

Formula over a window of N daily closes:

Convexity = [ (P1+PN)/2 − mean(P1..PN) ] / [ (P1+PN)/2 ]

- Convexity is positive (a convex path) when the midpoint of the first and last close is above the average close. Convexity is negative (a concave path) when it is below.
- The baseline window is one calendar month of daily closes.
- The paper uses price levels rather than returns. They adjust for splits but not cash dividends.
- Standardizing by the average price instead of the midpoint gives "nearly identical" results.

Aggregate test:
- Sample is S&P 500 (CRSP spindx), July 1963 to December 2022.
- They regress the next-month S&P 500 log excess return on standardized convexity, with Newey-West standard errors (about 6 lags).
- A one-SD increase in convexity goes with a **0.40% lower next-month return**. In-sample R² is **0.85%**, the highest of the 15 Welch-Goyal predictors.
- Out-of-sample R² is **0.39% to 0.61%**, while nearly all other predictors are negative.
- Excluding March and April 2020 raises the coefficient by about 50% and roughly quadruples the out-of-sample R².

Firm-level test:
- Sample is CRSP common stocks from July 1963, sorted into value-weighted quintiles monthly.
- Low-minus-high convexity earns **0.84%/month (t = 6.29)**, with a monthly Sharpe of **0.23 (about 0.80 annualized)**.
- In recessions it earns 1.60%/month (t = 3.18), Sharpe 0.34.
- Factor-model alphas are **0.78% to 0.97%/month** (t = 4.60 to 5.69).
- Shorter horizons:
  - 10-day look-back with a 10-day hold: **0.64% per 10 days (t = 7.31)**.
  - 5-day look-back with a 5-day hold: **0.37% per 5 days (t = 4.94)**, which annualizes to about 16% or more.
- Robustness:
  - The result holds in a July 1926 to June 1963 out-of-period sample, among large firms, excluding January, and on bid-ask midpoint prices.
  - It is not short-term reversal: a double sort on convexity and return shows this.
  - The Fama-MacBeth coefficient stays at t of about −9 to −11 with controls.
- Mechanism: survey expectations (21-day convexity) are positively related to convexity, which the authors read as overextrapolation.

Tunable parameters:
- Look-back N (5, 10, 21 days or bars).
- Standardization denominator.
- Holding period.
- Signal threshold (quintile cut versus a z-score of the series' own history).
- Price versus log-price.

**2. January Barometer, "long/T-bill" rule (Cooper, McConnell & Ovtchinnikov)**

Source for this section: [2009 PDF](https://business.purdue.edu/faculty/mcconnell/publications/Whats-the-best-way.pdf).

- Rule: always be long in January. If the January market return is positive, stay long February to December. If it is negative, hold T-bills February to December.
- The alternatives tested were long-only, long/short (short after a negative January), T-bill only, and January plus T-bills.
- Over 152 years (1857 to 2008), long/T-bill has annual SD **16.8%** and Sharpe **0.38**, against SD **19.2%** and Sharpe **0.31** for passive long-only. It also has the highest terminal wealth.
- Shorting after a negative January was "disastrous" because some 11-month bull runs followed negative Januarys.
- The 2006 JFE paper covers 1857 to 2003 with focus on 1940 to 2003. The predictive power of January survives controls for macro and business-cycle variables, the presidential cycle and sentiment, and appears in large and small stocks and in value and growth ([SUFE summary of JFE 2006](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/357472)).
- Tunable: which month (or first N days) acts as the barometer, the threshold (0 or another cutoff), and the hold-or-flat versus short choice.

**3. Turn of the month (McConnell & Xu)**

- Returns over the **four-day turn-of-month window** are well above other days. For 1926 to 2005 the TOM excess return averages about **0.15%/day versus −0.001%** on other days. In the authors' words, investors "received no reward for bearing market risk except at turns of the month" ([CFA Institute abstract](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2008/equity-returns-at-the-turn-of-the-month); [econpapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:taf:ufajxx:v:64:y:2008:i:2:p:49-64)).
- The effect is present in 31 of 35 countries. It is not confined to small or low-price stocks or to year- or quarter-ends, and it is not explained by month-end volume or fund flows ([CFA Institute](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2008/equity-returns-at-the-turn-of-the-month)).
- The 1987 to 2005 working-paper version gives 0.14% versus −0.01% (value-weighted) ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=819)).
- Quantpedia catalogs it as "turn of the month in equity indexes" ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/turn-of-the-month-in-equity-indexes)).

**4. Absolute-strength momentum (Gulen & Petkova, SSRN 2015/2018)**

- Winners and losers are classified by **fixed historical cutoffs of the cumulative-return distribution** rather than by cross-sectional rank ([Idea Farm summary](https://theideafarm.com/portfolio-management/absolute-strength-exploring-momentum-in-stock-returns/); [EconBiz record](https://www.econbiz.de/Record/absolute-strength-exploring-momentum-in-stock-returns/10012937096)).
- Stocks with significantly large past gains keep gaining, and stocks with significantly large past losses keep losing.
- Reported performance for an 11-1-1 design (11-month formation, 1-month skip, 1-month hold) is about **2.42%/month risk-adjusted, 1965 to 2014** ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/2015/10/absolute-strength-momentum-investing-strategy/)). Another summary gives 2.50%/month and Sharpe 0.34 for 1965 to 2016.
- Summaries say it "completely explain[s]" relative-strength and time-series momentum returns, and that it avoids the severe crashes of crisis periods.
- A later study (Yang & Zhang 2019, JFM) finds that *extreme* absolute-strength stocks are more prone to momentum reversals ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v44y2019icp71-90.html)).
- Tunable: the breakpoint percentiles of the historical distribution (for example top and bottom 10%), the formation, skip and hold lengths, and how much history defines the breakpoints.

**5. Market-state-conditioned momentum (Cooper, Gutierrez & Hameed 2004)**

- Market state is "UP" if the lagged 36-month market return is at or above zero, and "DOWN" otherwise.
- For 1929 to 1995, momentum earns about **0.93%/month after UP states** and **−0.37% after DOWN states**, and UP-state momentum later reverses ([typeset copy / summary](https://typeset.io/pdf/market-states-and-momentum-4wtc7g4rqv.pdf)).
- A Spanish-market replication (Muga & Santamaría 2006) found momentum after both states, which contradicts the paper ([EFMA paper](https://www.efmaefm.org/0EFMSYMPOSIUM/2006/papers/87-EFM06%20MUGA%20MOMENTUM-MARKETSTATES.doc)).
- Tunable: state look-back (12, 24 or 36 months) and the state threshold.

**6. Night versus day returns (Cliff, Cooper & Gulen)**

- Using transaction data, they split US equity returns into close-to-open (night) and open-to-close (day) returns.
- The US equity premium over the prior decade was earned essentially **entirely overnight**, while intraday returns were weak or negative ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/calendar-effects/buy-at-the-close-and-sell-at-the-open); [Albert Bridge Capital](https://www.albertbridgecapital.com/drew-views/2019/4/9/groundhog-day-and-overnight-returns)).
- Replications:
  - Kelly & Clark (2011, J. Asset Management) confirm the pattern ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/pal/assmgt/v12y2011i2d10.1057_jam.2011.2.html)).
  - An ETF study reports the effect weakening or disappearing from 2006 ([CXO/Lafayette results in search](https://dspace.lafayette.edu/handle/10385/1012)).

**7. Extrapolation-conditioned valuation timing (Cassella & Gulen 2018, RFS 31(11) 4345–4397)**

- DOX, the degree of extrapolation estimated from survey expectations, varies over time ([econpapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:31:y:2018:i:11:p:4345-4397.); [SUFE](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/353203)).
- High price-scaled variables (low D/P and similar) predict lower year-ahead returns **only when DOX is high**.

**8. Contrarians/extrapolators (Atmaz et al. 2024)**

- Surveys show roughly 60% of investors are extrapolators and about 20% each are contrarians or unbiased ([EBSCO summary](https://www.ebsco.com/articles/business-and-management/e03f662e-fc3e-54a5-912e-b26ed93e3ce5/contrarians-extrapolators-and-stock-market-momentum-and-reversal)).
- Contrarians' beliefs are less persistent. The resulting equilibrium model produces short-run market momentum and long-run reversal, and the empirical tests support it ([SUFE](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/387528)).

**9. Option-implied volatility gap (Goyal & Saretto)**

- Stocks are sorted on historical realized volatility minus ATM implied volatility, and option portfolios (straddles) are traded on the sort.
- Reported returns are 18.2% for liquid-option stocks and 27.7% for illiquid ones ([Purdue CIBER WP](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1054&context=ciberwp)).

### Inferences
- Convexity is the most completely specified signal. It needs only closing prices, has a single look-back parameter, and is already validated on the S&P 500 index in time series.

### Gaps
- I did not see the Gulen & Petkova breakpoint definitions in the primary source. The Alpha Architect page returned 403, so the numbers come from search snippets and summaries.
- I did not see the full text of Cliff/Cooper/Gulen (sample years, annualized night/day figures) or of the 2004 market-states paper.
- From primary text I could not confirm which exact days make up McConnell & Xu's four-day TOM window. The standard convention, from Lakonishok & Smidt, is the last trading day of the month plus the first three of the next, but treat this as unverified.
- I did not find the precise DOX trading rule or its out-of-sample numbers.

## Can the signal be applied in time series to a single futures contract, rather than only as a cross-sectional stock sort? Explain how it could be adapted.

### Takeaway
Yes for most of them. Convexity, TOM, the January Barometer, night/day and market-state conditioning were all documented on the aggregate market, or are natively time series. They map directly onto ES/NQ/RTY/YM and can be tried on CL/GC/SI/ZN/6E/6J. Absolute-strength momentum can be run as a time-series rule against the instrument's own return history. Saretto's option signals and DOX need data beyond 1-minute OHLCV.

### Cited Findings
- Convexity was tested in time series on the S&P 500 index, at one-month horizon, with positive out-of-sample R² ([JFQA PDF](https://jfqa.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/25822_Price-path-convexity.pdf)).
- TOM, the January Barometer and night/day are all market-level effects ([CFA Institute](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2008/equity-returns-at-the-turn-of-the-month); [Purdue PDF](https://business.purdue.edu/faculty/mcconnell/publications/Whats-the-best-way.pdf); [CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/calendar-effects/buy-at-the-close-and-sell-at-the-open)).
- A futures-market thesis reports that trading and non-trading period price patterns differ significantly ([Aalto thesis](https://epub.lib.aalto.fi/en/ethesis/id/13246)).

### Inferences
Suggested adaptations for 1-minute OHLCV resampled to the needed frequency:

**Convexity on ES/NQ**
- Compute CON on the last N daily RTH closes (N = 5, 10 or 21), or intraday on N bars (for example 60 × 5-minute bars).
- Turn it into a trade signal by z-scoring against a rolling history, then go short or underweight when CON is high and long when it is low. Hold for N.
- The firm-level results suggest a 5 to 10 day horizon is viable. Intraday versions are untested extrapolation.
- Because it is level-based, use the continuous back-adjusted contract carefully. Ratio adjustment is preferable so that the midpoint-versus-average comparison is not distorted by roll gaps.

**TOM**
- Long ES/NQ/RTY/YM from the close of trading day −2 to the close of trading day +3 (or −1 to +3). Stay flat otherwise.
- Tune the entry and exit day offsets.
- Other instruments (ZN, GC) are untested.

**January Barometer**
- Only one decision a year, so about 15 observations exist in the 1-minute history. It cannot be validated statistically on intraday data alone.
- It could be generalized to "first N trading days of month predicts the rest of the month", which is a speculative adaptation.

**Night/day**
- Hold ES/NQ from the RTH close (16:00 ET) to the RTH open (09:30 ET), flat in RTH, or condition intraday strategies on session.
- Futures trade nearly 23 hours, so "night" should be defined by the cash-session boundaries.
- Gap risk is limited in futures because the instrument trades continuously.

**Absolute strength as time series**
- Take the instrument's own trailing 11-1 (or shorter) return, compare it with the percentile distribution of its own historical 11-month returns, and go long or short only when it falls above the top or below the bottom breakpoint. Flat otherwise.
- This is a thresholded TSMOM.

**Market-state filter**
- Apply as a regime switch: enable trend or momentum strategies only when the trailing 36-month (or 12-month) index return is at or above 0.

**Data flags**
- DOX and Atmaz et al. need survey expectations data (Gallup, AAII, Shiller).
- Goyal & Saretto need option implied volatility (OptionMetrics, or CME options on futures).
- Classic valuation predictors (D/P) need fundamentals.
- None of these work from OHLCV alone. An OHLCV-only proxy for the realized-implied gap is impossible without IV data. VIX could serve for ES only.

### Gaps
- I found no published test of price-path convexity on futures other than S&P 500 index levels, and no test of it at intraday frequency.
- I found no Purdue-authored evidence on these effects in commodities, rates or FX futures.

## How obscure is it compared with mainstream strategies (MA crossover, plain mean reversion, Donchian or noise-band breakouts, Bollinger/Keltner squeeze)?

### Takeaway
Price-path convexity is the least known and most novel signal. It was accepted in JFQA in 2025 and is distinct from reversal and trend rules. Absolute-strength thresholding and market-state conditioning are moderately known in quant circles. TOM, the January Barometer and the overnight effect are well known (Quantpedia, CXO, popular press), so they are mostly arbitraged or widely traded.

### Cited Findings
- Convexity is "not merely short-term return reversal in disguise." It survives controls for known predictors, microstructure frictions and illiquidity, and outperforms 15 standard aggregate predictors ([JFQA PDF](https://jfqa.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/25822_Price-path-convexity.pdf)).
- Follow-on uses so far are in analyst downgrades and fund flows, not trading-blog coverage ([EFMA 2024 paper](https://efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2024-Lisbon/papers/EFMA_full_paper316.pdf); [SMU SKBI paper](https://skbi.smu.edu.sg/sites/skbi.smu.edu.sg/files/Research/Beyond%20Returns%20-%20The%20Impact%20of%20Price%20Path%20Convexity%20on%20Mutual%20Fund%20Flows.pdf)).
- TOM is listed as a standard Quantpedia strategy ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/turn-of-the-month-in-equity-indexes)). The January Barometer has been in print since Hirsch in 1973 ([Purdue PDF](https://business.purdue.edu/faculty/mcconnell/publications/Whats-the-best-way.pdf)).
- Overnight returns are widely covered, for example by LuxAlgo and FastML, and an ETF study shows the effect decaying after 2006 ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/overnight-vs-intraday-returns.md); [Lafayette](https://dspace.lafayette.edu/handle/10385/1012)).
- Absolute strength has blog and podcast coverage (Alpha Architect, the "Papers with Backtest" podcast), but it is still an SSRN working paper ([Ausha podcast](https://podcast.ausha.co/papers-with-backtest-an-algorithmic-trading-journey/absolute-strength-momentum)).

### Inferences
- Convexity differs mechanically from the existing project families:
  - MA crossover and Donchian/noise-band breakouts condition on the *level or extreme* of price.
  - Plain mean reversion conditions on the *size* of a move.
  - Squeezes condition on *volatility compression*.
  - Convexity conditions on the *shape* (timing) of the move inside the window. Two windows with the same net return can carry opposite signals.
- That shape-based design makes it the best ablation candidate from this research. It also combines naturally as a filter on existing breakout or mean-reversion entries.
- Calendar effects (TOM, night/day) are cheap to test as overlays or filters on existing strategies, but have low novelty.

### Gaps
- I found no evidence about how much institutional futures capital trades convexity or absolute-strength rules.
- I found no transaction-cost or turnover analysis for convexity in the full text (a grep found none), so its net profitability at 5 to 10 day horizons on futures is unknown.
