# Purdue-Affiliated Research on Intraday, High-Frequency and Microstructure Trading Patterns

Scope note: 15 tool calls. SSRN, Cambridge Core full text and AgEcon Search PDFs returned 403 errors or empty pages, so many numbers below come from abstracts, Purdue repository pages or secondary summaries (CXO Advisory). Each bullet says which kind of source it rests on.

## Which Purdue-affiliated researchers (current or at the time of publication) have published on intraday or short-horizon return predictability or microstructure-based strategies?

### Takeaway
The clearest Purdue (Krannert/Daniels) contributions on short-horizon tradeable patterns are:
- Huseyin Gulen's overnight-vs-intraday ("night and day") decomposition with Cliff and Cooper.
- John McConnell and Wei Xu's turn-of-the-month effect.
- Michael Cooper's weekly price-and-volume filter-rule reversal, written while he was at Krannert.
- Sugato Chakravarty's order-flow and price-discovery work: Clean Sweep / ISOs, and stock vs option informed trading with Gulen.
- Purdue Agricultural Economics (Mindy Mallory and her students) on intraday grain and livestock futures microstructure.

I found no Purdue-authored paper on intraday momentum (first and last half-hour) or on FOMC/macro-announcement intraday drift.

## What exact rules define each strategy or signal, and which parameters could be tuned?

### Takeaway
The two calendar/session rules (night vs day, turn of month) are fully defined by clock or calendar windows and are trivially testable on futures. The Cooper filter rule is a weekly reversal conditioned on volume change. The Chakravarty papers are price-discovery measurements, not trading rules.

### Cited Findings
- **Night/day (Cliff, Cooper, Gulen)**
  - Session split: Night = 4:00 PM to 9:30 AM, AM = 9:30–10:30, Mid-day = 10:30–15:00, PM = 15:00–16:00 ET.
  - Implied strategy: hold from the close to the next open, versus holding open to close. — [CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/?p=1377)
- **Turn of month (McConnell and Xu)**
  - Window: the last trading day of month t through the first three trading days of month t+1, four days in total.
  - Comparison period: the other ~16 trading days.
  - Strategy: long the index during the window, flat otherwise. — [Purdue CIBER WP 43](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/ciberwp/43)
- **Cooper (1999) filter rule**: filter rules on lagged returns combined with lagged volume information.
  - Large past moves with *decreasing* volume reverse more strongly.
  - *Increasing*-volume stocks show weaker reversals and even positive autocorrelation.
  - Horizon is weekly. — [IDEAS abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v12y1999i4p901-35.html)
- **Clean Sweep**: classifies trades as ISO versus non-ISO. An ISO is a limit order that executes in one venue even when another venue shows a better quote, provided the sender also routes orders to those better-priced venues. — [Cambridge/JFQA abstract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/clean-sweep-informed-trading-through-intermarket-sweep-orders/3FCE622538CB57F8573091BFE46E91DA)
- **Chakravarty, Gulen, Mayhew**: uses a modified Hasbrouck (1995) information-share decomposition between stock and option quotes. — [Bohrium record](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/informed-trading-in-stock-and-option-markets/812353450569367552-10567)

### Inferences
Tunable parameters for a futures port:
- **Night/day:**
  - The definition of "open" and "close" (RTH 9:30/16:00 vs the CME 17:00 Globex reopen).
  - Splitting the night into Asia, Europe and pre-US segments.
  - A first-hour fade (short 9:30–10:30), since the paper attributes much of the effect to a high open that sells off in the first hour.
  - A day-of-week filter (Friday close to Monday open).
- **Turn of month:**
  - Window start offset (−2 to 0) and length (3 to 5 days).
  - Entry on the close of T−1 versus the open of T.
  - Combining with the overnight-only exposure inside the window (a natural intersection of the two Purdue findings).
- **Cooper filter:**
  - Lookback length (1 to 2 weeks, or adapted to days).
  - Return-size threshold.
  - Volume-growth threshold and its sign.
  - Holding period.
  - On a single futures contract this becomes a time-series rule: fade large k-day moves only when volume is falling.

### Gaps
- The exact Cooper thresholds (return filter percentages, volume-growth cutoffs, lookback weeks) and sample period could not be extracted. The full PDF fetch returned empty and SSRN/OUP were not reachable, so they need checking against the RFS text.
- I did not obtain McConnell and Xu's per-window return figures (for example, average TOM-window versus rest-of-month daily return).

## What did they find, on which assets (equities, ETFs, futures), and over what sample?

### Takeaway
- **Night/day:** US equity returns over 1993–2006 accrued overnight, while daytime returns were roughly zero or negative, including in index futures.
- **Turn of month:** essentially all of the market's excess return over 1926–2005 (and 1897–2005) came in the four-day window, across 31 of 35 countries.
- **Order flow:** ISO orders and the option market carry meaningful price-discovery share, but neither gives an OHLCV-testable rule.

### Cited Findings
- **Night/day (Cliff, Cooper, Gulen):**
  - Data: transaction-level data on S&P 500 stocks, 14 high-volume ETFs, equity indexes and equity index futures, 1993–2006. — [CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/?p=1377)
  - S&P 500 stocks: average overnight return 0.028%–0.048% per day versus a daytime average of −0.028% to +0.002%, depending on the averaging method. — [CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/?p=1377)
  - ETFs: the night-minus-day spread averaged 0.18% for QQQQ, 0.068% for SPY and 0.061% for DIA. Daytime returns were negative for 12 of the 14 ETFs. — [CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/?p=1377)
  - The effect is also reported in equity index futures; the summary gives no figures for them. — [CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/?p=1377)
  - Friday-close-to-Monday-open returns were strongly positive (0.042% for S&P 500 stocks), which reverses older weekend-effect results. — [CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/?p=1377)
  - Overnight returns had *lower* standard deviation than daytime returns. Earnings-announcement timing, ECN growth and decimalization do not explain the effect. — [CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/?p=1377)
  - Average first-hour (9:30–10:30) returns for S&P 500 stocks were −0.012% to −0.036%: high opening prices fall during the first hour. — [CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/?p=1377)
  - Later work cites the paper alongside reports of intraday return reversals. — [search summary of citing literature](https://cxoadvisory.com/calendar-effects/buy-at-the-close-and-sell-at-the-open)
- **Turn of month (McConnell and Xu):**
  - Data: CRSP daily returns. The effect persists in 1987–2005 after Lakonishok and Smidt's (1988) original DJIA 1897–1986 study.
  - Essentially all positive excess market return occurred in the four-day TOM window; the other 16 days earned no reward for market risk on average.
  - The effect is not confined to small or low-priced stocks, to December–January, or to quarter-ends, and is not explained by higher TOM volatility or by month-end buying pressure (volume, mutual-fund flows). — [Purdue CIBER WP 43](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/ciberwp/43)
  - Present in 31 of 35 countries. — [CFA Institute FAJ page](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2008/equity-returns-at-the-turn-of-the-month)
- **Cooper (1999):** weekly overreaction profits on large-cap NYSE and AMEX stocks. The sample was chosen to minimize concerns about time-varying risk premia and microstructure (bid-ask bounce). — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v12y1999i4p901-35.html)
- **Clean Sweep:**
  - ISOs were 46% of trades and 41% of volume in the sample.
  - ISOs have a significantly larger information share despite smaller trade size.
  - Post-trade returns suggest informed institutions are the main users. — [JFQA abstract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/clean-sweep-informed-trading-through-intermarket-sweep-orders/3FCE622538CB57F8573091BFE46E91DA)
- **Chakravarty, Gulen, Mayhew (2004):**
  - The option market contributes about 17–18% of price discovery, with information-share bounds reported as 17.46% (lower) and 18.29% (upper).
  - The share declined slightly over the sample.
  - It is related to option volume, spreads in both markets and underlying volatility, and it varies by moneyness.
  - These bounds come from an aggregator and need checking against the JF original. — [Bohrium](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/informed-trading-in-stock-and-option-markets/812353450569367552-10567)
- **Boehmer, Jones, Zhang (2008):**
  - Data: proprietary NYSE order data, 2000–2004.
  - Shorting is more than 12.9% of NYSE volume.
  - Heavily shorted stocks underperform, and daily short flow predicts negative returns. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v63y2008i2p491-527.html); [NY Fed copy](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/conference/2010/cb/Boehmer_Jones_Zhang1.pdf)
- **Ag futures liquidity (Purdue Ag Econ):**
  - Related ag-futures work found that corn bid-ask spreads are substantially larger during index-trader roll periods and on USDA report release days. — [search summary of NCCC-134 literature](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/285856/files/Shang_Mallory_Garcia_NCCC-134_2016.pdf)
  - Nearby corn contracts lead deferred contracts in price discovery; nearby contracts are less dominant in live cattle. — [Hu, Mallory, Serra, Garcia 2017](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/285866/files/Hu_Mallory_Serra_Garcia_NCCC-134_2017.pdf)

### Inferences
- The night/day finding is directly portable to ES, NQ, RTY and YM with 1-min bars: compute RTH close-to-open versus open-to-close.
- Its first-hour reversal component points to a concrete intraday rule: fade the opening gap during 9:30–10:30.
- No Sharpe ratios or t-stats for a traded strategy were found in the sources reached. The numbers are mean-return decompositions, not net-of-cost strategy results.
- The overnight-premium literature after 2006 (for example, the JFQA "market return around the clock" puzzle) suggests the effect persisted, but I did not verify post-2006 replication for futures specifically. — [JFQA "Market Return Around the Clock"](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/market-return-around-the-clock-a-puzzle/089E33AC0B4D3B9A02CBA31EDF6505B3)

### Gaps
- No Sharpe, hit-rate or t-stat values were retrieved for any of the papers; only mean returns and shares.
- Futures-specific night/day magnitudes from Cliff, Cooper and Gulen were not available in the summary.
- Out-of-sample replication of turn-of-month after 2005 was not checked in this session.

## Is the strategy relatively unknown or uncommon, and is it testable on US futures with 1-minute OHLCV?

### Takeaway
None of these is exotic in the practitioner world: the overnight premium and turn-of-month are widely known calendar effects. Still, both differ structurally from MA, mean-reversion, Donchian, noise-band and squeeze systems, because they are session/calendar exposure rules. The Cooper volume-conditioned reversal is less commonly used and works on OHLCV. The ISO and option-information-share signals need data beyond OHLCV.

### Cited Findings
- The overnight-vs-intraday pattern is widely discussed by practitioners (for example, CXO's "buy at the close and sell at the open" coverage), so it is not obscure. — [CXO calendar effects](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/calendar-effects/buy-at-the-close-and-sell-at-the-open)
- The turn-of-month effect dates back to Lakonishok and Smidt (1988) and is a well-known calendar anomaly; McConnell and Xu's contribution is showing its dominance and persistence. — [Purdue CIBER WP 43](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/ciberwp/43)
- ISO classification requires trade-condition flags (TAQ), so it cannot be built from OHLCV. — [JFQA abstract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/clean-sweep-informed-trading-through-intermarket-sweep-orders/3FCE622538CB57F8573091BFE46E91DA)
- Stock/option information share requires synchronized stock and option quotes. — [Bohrium](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/informed-trading-in-stock-and-option-markets/812353450569367552-10567)
- Short-flow signals need proprietary or Reg SHO short-sale data and apply to single stocks, not futures. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v63y2008i2p491-527.html)

### Inferences
Testability on the user's futures list with 1-min OHLCV:
- **Night/day hold plus first-hour fade (Cliff, Cooper, Gulen):** testable on ES, NQ, RTY and YM. It may also be informative on GC, SI, CL, ZN, 6E and 6J as a cross-asset check; the paper is equity-only. Moderately well known.
- **Turn of month (McConnell and Xu):** testable on all contracts with daily or 1-min bars. Well known, but rarely combined with intraday exposure (TOM × overnight-only).
- **Cooper volume-filter reversal:** testable using bar volume. Futures volume has roll artifacts, so use the continuous front month with roll-adjusted volume. Uncommon in retail systems.
- **USDA-report liquidity effects (Mallory):** only relevant to grains, which are not on the user's list. Spreads need bid/ask data.
- **Needs data beyond OHLCV:** ISO (TAQ condition codes), option information share (option quotes), short-sale flow (Reg SHO).

### Gaps
- No Purdue-authored paper on FOMC or macro-release intraday trading, intraday momentum, end-of-day effects or HFT liquidity provision in equity index futures was found in this search budget.
