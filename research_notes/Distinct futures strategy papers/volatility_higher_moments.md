# Volatility-Driven and Higher-Moment Strategies for Liquid US Futures (Intraday OHLCV)

Scope: papers whose signal comes from realized volatility, realized semivariance and higher moments, jumps, or volatility regimes, and that can be built from 1-minute OHLCV on ES, NQ, RTY, YM, CL, GC, 6E, 6J, ZN (2018-01 to 2026-10). This excludes squeeze/compression breakouts, which the user already tests.

Research budget note: I read the full text of Harvey et al. (2018), Wang & Yan (2021), Bollerslev (2022) and Le, Kourtis & Markellos (2023). For Moreira & Muir (2017), Cederburg et al. (2020), Amaya et al. (2015), Bongaerts et al. (2020), Barroso & Detzel (2021), Xiao, Yin & Zhao (2020), Fernandez-Perez et al. (2018) and Bollerslev, Tauchen & Zhou (2009), I only saw abstracts and secondary summaries. Rule details for those come from the abstracts or the summaries, and are flagged where that matters.

## Q1. Candidate papers and exact signal construction (sampling, lookback, entry/exit, holding, instruments, sample, reported performance, costs)

### Takeaway
The best-documented and most implementable family is **volatility-managed / volatility-targeted exposure**: Moreira & Muir 2017; Harvey et al. 2018, which uses 5-minute S&P 500 futures data; and the downside-volatility variant of Wang & Yan 2021. Evidence on these is strongest for equity index exposure, and that is mainly an **overlay**. **Realized semivariance** (Bollerslev 2022) and **post-jump reversal** (Xiao, Yin & Zhao 2020) are the most promising standalone directional ideas that are new to the user. The return predictability they report is statistically weak or untested net of costs. **Realized skewness** is only documented convincingly in the stock cross-section (Amaya et al. 2015) and the commodity-futures cross-section using daily data (Fernandez-Perez et al. 2018). I found no paper showing that time-series realized skewness predicts index-futures returns.

### Cited Findings

**Paper 1: Moreira & Muir (2017), "Volatility-Managed Portfolios," Journal of Finance 72(4), 1611-1644.** NBER WP: https://www.nber.org/papers/w22208 ; published PDF: https://amoreira2.github.io/alan-moreira.github.io/VolPortfolios_published.pdf
- Rule: scale each month's factor return by the inverse of the **previous month's realized variance**, so risk exposure falls after high-variance months and rises after calm months. Weight w_t = c / sigma^2_{t-1}, where sigma^2_{t-1} is realized variance in month t-1 from daily returns, and c is set so the managed strategy's full-sample volatility equals buy-and-hold volatility. — [NBER w22208](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22208); weight formula as described in a secondary source, [arXiv 2003.00656](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.00656). I did not verify the exact equation against the published PDF.
- c is not known in real time. Sharpe ratios and appraisal ratios do not depend on c. — [Wang & Yan 2021, describing the MM construction](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/Downside.pdf)
- Moreira & Muir test trading costs of 1, 10 and 14 bp, where 1 bp is a reasonable cost only for the market factor. — [Wang & Yan 2021](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/Downside.pdf)

**Paper 2: Harvey, Hoyle, Korgaonkar, Rattray, Sargaison & Van Hemert (2018), "The Impact of Volatility Targeting," Journal of Portfolio Management 45(1), 14-33. DOI 10.3905/jpm.2018.45.1.014.** PDF: https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf
- This is the closest match to the user's setup: it uses **S&P 500 futures and 10-year Treasury futures with 5-minute intraday data from 1988**. — [Harvey et al. PDF](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf)
- Intraday volatility estimator: 5-minute returns over the **9:15 a.m. to 2:00 p.m. Chicago window** only. Squared 5-minute returns are summed to a daily realized variance, the daily values are averaged with **exponentially decaying weights**, and the square root is taken. Adding the squared overnight return gave "slightly less persistence." — [Harvey et al.](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf)
- Daily-data estimator: zero-mean standard deviation of daily returns with exponential weights. Half-lives are tested up to 90 days, and 270 trading days of warm-up are required. Equal-weight rolling windows and 3-day or 5-day overlapping returns give similar results. — [Harvey et al.](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf)
- Target volatility is 10% annualized. The scaled return is r_t x (sigma_target / sigma_hat_{t-1}). — [Harvey et al.](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf)
- U.S. equities 1927-2017: the Sharpe ratio rises from **0.40 unscaled to 0.48-0.51 scaled**, with little sensitivity to the half-life. Gross and net Sharpe ratios are the same at the reported precision. A regression of scaled daily returns (20-day half-life) on unscaled returns gives an intercept of 0.64 bp per day, t = 3.05. — [Harvey et al.](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf)
- S&P 500 futures 1988-2017: "The Sharpe ratio, vol of vol, and left tail (mean shortfall) all slightly improve when using intraday data (and comparing versions with a similar turnover value)." The exhibit's numbers did not survive PDF text extraction. — [Harvey et al.](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf)
- Across 60+ assets: volatility targeting consistently lowers vol of vol and the left tail. Sharpe gains are concentrated in risk assets (equities, credit). For bonds, currencies and commodities the effect on Sharpe is negligible. — [Harvey et al.](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf); [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/volatility-targeting-improves-risk-adjusted-returns/)
- In U.S. equities, mean returns are similar across prior-month volatility quintiles. In bonds, mean returns are much higher in the high-volatility quintile, which works against volatility targeting there. — [Harvey et al.](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf)

**Paper 3: Wang & Yan (2021), "Downside risk and the performance of volatility-managed portfolios," Journal of Banking & Finance 131, 106198.** PDF: https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/Downside.pdf
- Rule: f_down,t = (c~ / sigma_Down,t-1) x f_t. Here **sigma_Down,t = sqrt(sum of squared daily returns where the return is < 0, within month t)**. If month t has fewer than 3 negative days, use the negative days of months t and t-1. Total volatility uses all daily squared returns. Note that the weight is 1/sigma (volatility), not 1/sigma^2. — [Wang & Yan](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/Downside.pdf)
- Result: scaling by downside volatility performs significantly better than scaling by total volatility in spanning regressions, direct Sharpe comparisons and real-time strategies. The decomposition attributes the gain mainly to **return timing: downside volatility negatively predicts future returns**. — [Wang & Yan](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/Downside.pdf)
- Test assets: 9 equity factors, including the market, and 94 anomalies. Samples end December 2018. — [Wang & Yan](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/Downside.pdf)
- Costs: at 1, 10 and 14 bp most spanning alphas stay positive. At 25 and 50 bp most total-vol-managed factors turn negative. — [Wang & Yan](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/Downside.pdf)
- Real-time test: 120-month training window with an expanding window, mean-variance combination of the managed and unmanaged factor, gamma = 5, leverage cap of 5. **Fixed-weight combinations, for example 50% managed and 50% unmanaged, beat estimated "optimal" weights** because they avoid parameter instability. — [Wang & Yan](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/Downside.pdf)

**Paper 4: Bollerslev (2022), "Realized Semi(co)variation: Signs That All Volatilities are Not Created Equal," Journal of Financial Econometrics 20(2), 219-252 (SoFiE Presidential Address). DOI 10.1093/jjfinec/nbab025.** PDF: https://public.econ.duke.edu/~boller/Papers/Semi_JFEC_2022.pdf
- Definitions: RV+ is the sum of squared positive intraday returns and RV- is the sum of squared negative intraday returns. They are computed from SPY 5-minute returns, 2002-2020, 4,783 daily observations. — [Bollerslev 2022](https://public.econ.duke.edu/~boller/Papers/Semi_JFEC_2022.pdf)
- Return predictability (Table 1): daily, weekly (5-day) and monthly (21-day) SPY returns are regressed on sqrt(RV), and separately on sqrt(RV+) and sqrt(RV-). Coefficients on **sqrt(RV+) are negative** at all horizons but not significant. Coefficients on **sqrt(RV-) are positive** at all horizons and significant only at the monthly horizon: coefficient 0.057, Newey-West SE 0.027, Hodrick t = 2.145. Total RV is insignificant. The author warns that **R^2 is close to zero** and that economic significance should be "interpreted with some caution." — [Bollerslev 2022](https://public.econ.duke.edu/~boller/Papers/Semi_JFEC_2022.pdf)
- Breckenfelder & Tedongap (2012, unpublished) found that the **up-minus-down realized semivariance** of the market negatively predicts future market returns. — [cited in Bollerslev 2022](https://public.econ.duke.edu/~boller/Papers/Semi_JFEC_2022.pdf)
- Volatility forecasting: the semivariance HAR model (SHAR) has R^2 = 0.624 against 0.594 for the plain HAR model for SPY over 2002-2020. Higher downside (upside) semivariance means higher (lower) future volatility. — [Bollerslev 2022](https://public.econ.duke.edu/~boller/Papers/Semi_JFEC_2022.pdf); [Patton & Sheppard working paper](https://public.econ.duke.edu/~ap172/Patton_Sheppard_Realized_Semivariance_7oct11.pdf)
- Most of the return predictability in the variance risk premium can be traced to "bad" volatility. — [Bollerslev 2022](https://public.econ.duke.edu/~boller/Papers/Semi_JFEC_2022.pdf)
- Note on the sign conflict: Bollerslev finds that downside semivariance predicts **higher** market returns over the next month, a risk-premium sign. Wang & Yan find that downside volatility predicts **lower** factor returns, a return-timing sign. The assets, frequencies and normalizations differ. — [Bollerslev 2022](https://public.econ.duke.edu/~boller/Papers/Semi_JFEC_2022.pdf) vs [Wang & Yan 2021](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/Downside.pdf)

**Paper 5: Xiao, Yin & Zhao (2020), "Jumps, News, and Subsequent Return Dynamics: An Intraday Study," Journal of Financial Research 43(3), 705-731.** https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfnres/v43y2020i3p705-731.html (abstract-level only)
- Jumps are detected in high-frequency prices of ETFs tracking broad U.S. equity indexes. 43% of jumps are linked to macro news and 57% are not. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfnres/v43y2020i3p705-731.html); [vLex abstract](https://law-journals-books.vlex.com/vid/jumps-news-and-subsequent-855590156)
- **No-news jumps reverse strongly for at least 60 minutes**, much more than an ordinary 15-minute interval without a jump. Scheduled-news jumps reverse. **Unscheduled-news jumps show momentum.** Negative jumps reverse more strongly than positive jumps. — [vLex abstract](https://law-journals-books.vlex.com/vid/jumps-news-and-subsequent-855590156)
- Related context on index futures: over 60% of S&P 500 futures jumps at 10:05 a.m. are linked to 10:00 a.m. macro announcements, and news is absorbed in under one minute (Miao, Ramchander & Zumwalt, JFM 2014). The 60% figure comes from an Academia.edu summary and is unverified. — [Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/17999424/S_and_P_500_index_futures_price_jumps_and_macroeconomic_news)
- Bates (JF 2019): intraday jumps in S&P 500 futures (1983-2008) are typically small. Self-exciting, short-lived volatility spikes describe intraday and daily returns better. — [Bates 2019](https://www.biz.uiowa.edu/faculty/dbates/papers/BATES_JF_2019.pdf)

**Paper 6: Amaya, Christoffersen, Jacobs & Vasquez (2015), "Does Realized Skewness Predict the Cross-Section of Equity Returns?" Journal of Financial Economics 118(1), 135-167.** https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:118:y:2015:i:1:p:135-167 ; WP: https://repec.econ.au.dk/repec/creates/rp/13/rp13_41.pdf
- Weekly realized skewness and kurtosis are computed from intraday returns. Buying the lowest realized-skewness decile and shorting the highest earns **19 bp the following week (t = 3.70)** in the JFE version, and 24 bp per week (t = 3.65) in the 2013 working paper. Realized kurtosis has a positive but not always significant relation. Realized volatility shows no strong relation. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:118:y:2015:i:1:p:135-167); [CREATES WP](https://repec.econ.au.dk/repec/creates/rp/13/rp13_41.pdf)
- This is a **cross-sectional stock** result. With only about 9 futures it cannot be replicated directly. A time-series version for index futures is not documented in anything I found. A search for intraday realized skewness predicting next-day S&P futures returns returned no direct study. — [search summary; closest result is Zhang, Jondeau, Wang & Yan, JFM 2020, which uses average monthly stock skewness](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v40y2020i11p1648-1664.html)

**Paper 7: Fernandez-Perez, Frijns, Fuertes & Miffre (2018), "The Skewness of Commodity Futures Returns," Journal of Banking & Finance 86, 143-158.** Accepted version: https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/17843/
- Long the commodity futures with the most negative skewness and short those with the most positive skewness. Excess returns survive standard risk factors, and a tradeable skewness factor prices the commodity cross-section. The authors explain it with skewness preference under cumulative prospect theory plus selective hedging. — [City Research Online](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/17843/); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jbfina:v:86:y:2018:i:c:p:143-158)
- The paper uses daily-return skewness over a lookback window across about 27 commodities. Exact lookback and holding period are in the PDF, which I did not read. The user's universe has only CL and GC from commodities, so a cross-sectional version would mix asset classes.

**Paper 8 (conditional): Bollerslev, Tauchen & Zhou (2009), "Expected Stock Returns and Variance Risk Premia," Review of Financial Studies 22(11), 4463-4492.** https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=948309 ; Fed WP: https://www.federalreserve.gov/Pubs/Feds/2007/200711/200711pap.pdf
- VRP = implied variance (VIX^2) minus realized variance. It explains a "nontrivial fraction" of post-1990 aggregate stock returns, and high VRP predicts high returns. Predictability is **strongest at the quarterly horizon**. The results depend on using model-free implied variance and **realized variance from high-frequency intraday data** rather than daily data. — [Fed FEDS 2007-11](https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/feds/2007/200711/200711abs.html); [Duke Scholars](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/732839)
- **Data availability:** Cboe posts free daily VIX history (close from 1990, OHLC from 1992) and other volatility indices including VIX9D on its historical-data page. A VIX3M daily CSV from 2007 is reported at cdn.cboe.com/api/global/us_indices/daily_prices/VIX3M_History.csv; that link comes from a third party and I did not verify it. Tick-level VIX futures data is paid (Cboe DataShop). — [Cboe historical data](https://www.cboe.com/tradable-products/vix/vix-historical-data/); [Macroption VIX3M](https://www.macroption.com/vix3m/); [Cboe DataShop](https://datashop.cboe.com/volatility-index-futures-data)
- A VRP proxy for ES is therefore feasible: free daily VIX^2/12 minus 5-minute RV of ES computed from the user's Databento bars.
- VIX term structure as a contrarian timing indicator for equities is supported by Fassas & Hourvouliades (JRFM 2019). — [MDPI JRFM 12(3):113](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/jrfm/jrfm-12-00113/article_deploy/jrfm-12-00113-v2.pdf)

**Paper 9 (variant of Paper 1): Bongaerts, Kang & van Dijk (2020), "Conditional Volatility Targeting," Financial Analysts Journal 76(4), 54-71.** https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2020/conditional-volatility-targeting
- Conventional volatility targeting fails to consistently improve performance in global equity markets and can produce markedly larger drawdowns. The conditional version **adjusts exposure only in extreme high-volatility and low-volatility states**, and otherwise holds unit exposure. It improves Sharpe ratios in all markets tested (conventional: 8 of 10), lowers drawdowns and tail risk, and has low turnover and leverage. Exact regime thresholds are behind the paywall. — [CFA Institute](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2020/conditional-volatility-targeting); [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/conditional-volatility-targeting/)

### Inferences
Suggested implementations on the user's data. These are my own proposals and are not from the papers.
- **VT-1 (Moreira-Muir or Harvey style, overlay):** daily RV_d = sum of squared 5-minute log returns in RTH. ES RTH is 08:30-15:00 CT; Harvey uses 09:15-14:00 CT. Forecast sigma_hat_t = sqrt(EWMA_halflife(RV_d)), with an optional variant that adds the squared overnight return. Position_t = min(L_max, sigma_target / sigma_hat_{t-1}). Rebalance daily or monthly. Apply to buy-and-hold ES/NQ/RTY/YM, and to the user's existing strategy P&L streams.
- **VT-2 (Wang-Yan downside, overlay):** replace RV with RV- (squared negative 5-minute or daily returns) and scale by 1/sigma_down. Test a 50/50 fixed blend with the unmanaged position, following their real-time result.
- **VT-3 (Bongaerts conditional, overlay):** exposure = 1 unless sigma_hat is in the top or bottom X% of its trailing distribution, in which case scale down or up.
- **SV-1 (semivariance timing, standalone):** signal = (RV+ - RV-)/RV over the past d days, from 5-minute bars. Go long ES/NQ when the signal is low (downside volatility dominated) and flat or short when it is high. Hold 5-21 days. Expect a weak signal given R^2 near zero.
- **JR-1 (jump reversal, standalone intraday):** flag a 1-5 minute bar as a jump when |r| > k x local volatility. Local volatility comes from bipower variation over a trailing window, which is the Lee-Mykland-style approach common in this literature; the exact detector in Xiao et al. is unverified. Exclude bars within plus or minus N minutes of scheduled macro releases. Use 8:30 and 10:00 ET as a calendar proxy, because the user has no news feed. Fade the jump and hold 15-60 minutes. Treat negative jumps separately because the paper says they reverse more strongly.
- **VRP-1 (standalone, needs free Cboe VIX daily):** VRP_t = VIX_t^2/12 - RV_t(past 21 days, 5-minute ES). Long ES when VRP is in the top tercile and flat otherwise. Hold 1-3 months.
- I would deprioritize realized skewness for time-series index-futures timing because of the missing evidence. A cross-sectional realized-skewness rank across the 9 futures is possible but thinly supported.

### Gaps
- I did not read the exact jump-detection statistic, threshold, sampling frequency or ETF list in Xiao, Yin & Zhao (2020), nor whether they report trading-strategy returns net of spreads. I only had abstract access.
- No exact Sharpe numbers for S&P 500 futures with intraday-vol scaling: the Harvey et al. Exhibit 8 numbers did not extract from the PDF.
- I found no peer-reviewed paper on **overnight gap versus realized volatility** signals, and no paper on **HAR-RV forecast-based directional timing**. HAR and SHAR appear here only as volatility-forecasting inputs; the R^2 figures are from Bollerslev 2022.
- The Bongaerts et al. thresholds and the Moreira-Muir published equations were not verified from full text.
- No source on whether the realized-skewness effect exists in the time series of index futures at daily or weekly horizons. Le, Kourtis & Markellos (JFM 2023) compare skewness models for index portfolio choice over 2000-2019 and find option-implied skewness beats return-based skewness. Their best model needs options data. — [Le et al. 2023](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/91269/7/Le_etal_2023_JFuturesMarkets.pdf)

## Q2. Post-publication and out-of-sample evidence (especially 2018+)

### Takeaway
Out-of-sample and post-publication evidence for **volatility-managed portfolios is mixed to negative across factors, but the equity market factor is the most robust survivor**. Several critiques (Cederburg et al. 2020; Barroso & Detzel 2021; Bongaerts et al. 2020; the Liu-Tang-Zhou look-ahead critique) say plain Moreira-Muir scaling does not reliably beat buy-and-hold in real time. Downside-vol and conditional (extremes-only) variants hold up better. For semivariance, jump-reversal and skewness signals I found no post-2018 replication on futures.

### Cited Findings
- Cederburg, O'Doherty, Wang & Yan (2020), JFE 138(1), 95-117: across **103 equity strategies**, real-time out-of-sample volatility-managed versions generally earn **lower certainty-equivalent returns and Sharpe ratios** than unmanaged ones. The authors blame structural instability in the spanning regressions. — [author PDF](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:138:y:2020:i:1:p:95-117)
- One summary reports that managed versions outperformed in 53 of 103 cases, and that only 8 had consistently higher Sharpe. This is a secondary source. — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/the-performance-of-volatility-managed-portfolios/)
- Barroso & Detzel (2021), JFE 140(3), 744-767: after transaction costs, volatility management of factors **other than the market** mostly yields zero alpha and lower Sharpe, even with six cost-mitigation techniques. The managed **market** portfolio survives costs but outperforms mainly in high-sentiment periods. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:140:y:2021:i:3:p:744-767); [DeMiguel et al. JF 2024 citing it](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/3716/1/The%20Journal%20of%20Finance%20-%202024%20-%20DeMIGUEL%20-%20A%20Multifactor%20Perspective%20on%20Volatility%E2%80%90Managed%20Portfolios.pdf)
- Wang & Yan (2021): consistent with Barroso & Detzel, at 25-50 bp costs most managed factors lose their alpha. Downside-vol scaling and **fixed-weight blends** improve real-time performance. Samples run to December 2018. — [Wang & Yan](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/Downside.pdf)
- A later paper argues that the Harvey et al. scaling constant is set ex post, which introduces look-ahead bias. An implementable version does not consistently improve risk-adjusted performance in international equities. — [search summary of Bongaerts et al. / related critique, core.ac.uk](https://core.ac.uk/download/328853217.pdf). Attribution is not fully verified.
- Bongaerts, Kang & van Dijk (2020): conventional targeting improves Sharpe in 8 of 10 equity markets but can raise drawdowns. The conditional version improves Sharpe in all of them. — [CFA Institute](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2020/conditional-volatility-targeting); [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/conditional-volatility-targeting/)
- A Journal of Investment Strategies paper using futures in 9 markets (equities, government bonds, commodities) finds that volatility management **does not systematically raise long-run geometric returns but does reduce realized volatility**. I did not see authors or year. — [Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/node/7964235)
- Practitioner backtest, not peer-reviewed: Zarattini, Mele & Aziz (2025) use VIX/VIX3M slope plus a VRP sign to trade VIX ETPs. They report Sharpe 0.729 for Jan 2016 to Jul 2026 against 0.582 for SPY buy-and-hold, with a flat parameter surface (0.713-0.773). These are author backtests. — [QuantConnect](https://www.quantconnect.com/research/21143/harvesting-the-volatility-risk-premium-with-a-dual-vix-signal/)
- Bollerslev's (2022) semivariance return regressions run through 2020 on SPY. Only the monthly-horizon RV- coefficient is significant, and R^2 is near zero. — [Bollerslev 2022](https://public.econ.duke.edu/~boller/Papers/Semi_JFEC_2022.pdf)

### Inferences
- The user's 2018-2026 sample includes sharp volatility shocks: Feb 2018 (Volmageddon), Q4 2018, March 2020, 2022 and 2025. That makes it a good stress test for VT overlays. The critiques suggest the holdout should compare against unit exposure at the **same average leverage**, not at ex-post matched volatility, to avoid the look-ahead issue in c.
- Equity index futures are where volatility targeting has the strongest prior: Harvey et al., Barroso & Detzel's market survivor, Bongaerts et al. Expect little or no Sharpe gain on CL, GC, 6E, 6J and ZN. The likely gain there is tail and drawdown reduction only.

### Gaps
- No post-2018 out-of-sample test of jump reversal or realized-semivariance timing on index futures was found.
- Exact out-of-sample numbers from Cederburg et al. for the market factor specifically were not extracted.

## Q3. Natural ablation grids

### Takeaway
Each family has a small set of axes that the papers themselves vary: the volatility estimator (frequency, window or half-life, overnight in or out), the target or threshold, the leverage cap, the rebalance frequency, and for jumps the detection threshold and holding window.

### Cited Findings
- Harvey et al. vary the **EWMA half-life** (daily data, several values up to 90 days), **daily versus 5-minute intraday RV**, and **equal-weight window versus EWMA versus 3/5-day overlapping returns**. They evaluate at matched turnover, and results were insensitive to half-life (Sharpe 0.48-0.51). — [Harvey et al.](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf)
- Harvey et al. compare an RV window of **09:15-14:00 CT only versus adding the overnight return**. — [Harvey et al.](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf)
- Wang & Yan vary **total versus downside volatility**, **trading cost (1/10/14/25/50 bp)**, **real-time optimal versus fixed blend weights**, with a leverage cap of 5. — [Wang & Yan](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/Downside.pdf)
- Moreira & Muir scale by **variance (1/sigma^2)**. Wang & Yan and Harvey et al. scale by **volatility (1/sigma)**. — [Wang & Yan](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/Downside.pdf); [Harvey et al.](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf)
- Bongaerts et al.: **conditional (extremes-only) versus continuous scaling**. — [CFA Institute](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2020/conditional-volatility-targeting)
- Bollerslev: forecast horizons of **1, 5 and 21 days**, and the predictors **RV, RV+ and RV-** separately. — [Bollerslev 2022](https://public.econ.duke.edu/~boller/Papers/Semi_JFEC_2022.pdf)
- Xiao et al.: post-jump windows up to 60 minutes and beyond, plus splits by **news versus no news**, **scheduled versus unscheduled**, and **sign**. — [vLex abstract](https://law-journals-books.vlex.com/vid/jumps-news-and-subsequent-855590156)

### Inferences
Proposed grids (my own synthesis):
- **VT overlay:** estimator {daily close-to-close, 5m RTH RV, 5m RTH RV + overnight^2, 1m RV} x lookback {5, 10, 21, 63 days, or half-life 5/10/20/60} x scaling power {1/sigma, 1/sigma^2} x vol measure {total, downside RV-} x target {10%, 15%, or the asset's long-run median} x leverage cap {1.0, 1.5, 2, 3} x rebalance {daily, weekly, monthly, only if the change exceeds 10-25%} x form {continuous, conditional extremes at 10/20% tails} x blend {100% managed, 50/50}. Main metrics: Sharpe, maximum drawdown, left-tail mean shortfall, turnover, and profit factor (the user's standard).
- **Semivariance timing:** sampling {1m, 5m, 15m} x window {1, 5, 21 days} x signal {RV- level, RV-/RV, (RV+ - RV-)/RV} x entry rule {z-score > 1, top tercile} x hold {1, 5, 21 days} x direction {long-only, long/short}.
- **Jump reversal:** bar size {1m, 5m} x local-vol window {e.g. 30, 60, 120 bars of bipower variation} x threshold k {3, 4, 5, 6 sigma} x macro-time exclusion {on, off} x hold {15, 30, 60, 120 minutes, or until the session close} x sign {both, negative-only} x minimum jump size in ticks or percent.
- **VRP (ES only):** RV window {5, 21 days} x VRP threshold {>0, top tercile, z > 1} x hold {5, 21, 63 days}, plus a term-structure filter {VIX/VIX3M < 1}.

### Gaps
- None of the papers supplies a canonical jump threshold for 1-minute futures data, so that axis must be wide.

## Q4. Overlays versus standalone directional strategies

### Takeaway
Volatility targeting, downside-volatility scaling and conditional volatility targeting are **position-sizing overlays**. They can wrap the user's existing noise-band, mean-reversion, convexity and EMRT strategies, or plain long ES. Realized-semivariance timing, VRP timing, jump reversal and skewness sorts are **standalone directional signals**, although semivariance and VRP can also act as regime filters.

### Cited Findings
- Moreira & Muir, Harvey et al., Wang & Yan and Bongaerts et al. all rescale an existing return stream (a factor, an asset or a strategy) by inverse lagged volatility. They size positions and do not decide direction. — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22208); [Harvey et al.](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf); [Wang & Yan](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/Downside.pdf); [CFA Institute](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2020/conditional-volatility-targeting)
- Volatility scaling of momentum strategies roughly doubles the Sharpe of the original momentum strategy and reduces crashes (Barroso & Santa-Clara 2015; Daniel & Moskowitz 2016, as summarized in Wang & Yan). This is the precedent for applying the overlay to existing **strategy** P&L rather than to an asset. — [Wang & Yan](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/Downside.pdf)
- Barroso & Detzel: overlays on non-market factors are eaten by costs. Turnover matters. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:140:y:2021:i:3:p:744-767)
- Harvey et al.: volatility targeting introduces some momentum into risk assets, through the leverage effect, and reliably cuts vol of vol and the left tail even where Sharpe does not improve. — [Harvey et al.](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf)
- Directional signals: semivariance (Bollerslev 2022), VRP (Bollerslev, Tauchen & Zhou 2009), jump reversal (Xiao et al. 2020), and skewness long-short (Amaya et al. 2015; Fernandez-Perez et al. 2018). — sources as above.

### Inferences
- The highest-value first experiment is probably a **VT/downside-VT overlay applied to the user's already-validated strategies**, sized by the strategy's own trailing P&L volatility or by the underlying's 5-minute RV. That adds little new alpha risk, and futures make rebalancing cheap. For intraday strategies that are flat by the close, the overlay amounts to setting each day's contract count from sigma_hat_{t-1}, with no intramonth rebalancing cost.
- Jump reversal is the most "genuinely different" standalone intraday family. It is a high-frequency fade conditioned on a volatility-normalized shock, unlike the user's noise-band, RSI and squeeze rules. It needs careful slippage modeling because entries come right after violent bars.
- VRP and VIX-slope filters are ES/NQ-only (they need free Cboe daily VIX and VIX3M). They are best used as a regime gate on other ES strategies.

### Gaps
- No paper tests VT overlays on intraday breakout or mean-reversion strategies in futures specifically. That would be new territory for the user.
