# Test ICT's sweeps, not its folklore

Every ICT setup worth testing reduces to one question: **after price trades through an obvious prior high or low, does it reverse or continue?** The liquidity sweep is the only ICT element that has survived independent, baseline-controlled testing. Even that evidence disagrees on direction. A 25,000-config ES/NQ 1-minute Silver Bullet study found that requiring a sweep was the one rule that consistently added edge. A walk-forward FX study found swept levels predicted *continuation* in 65 of 66 folds. Everything else in the canon (fair value gaps, order blocks, OTE/Fibonacci, killzone hours, higher-timeframe bias and market-structure-shift filters) matches random or placebo entry once a proper null is applied. Your own funnel points the same way. The only open-session edge that survived your holdout is NQ noise-band, a *continuation* trade. Every fade-the-extreme family you have run (drift-burst, FX-fix, mean reversion) failed after 1 tick + $2.50/side. My recommendation is two grids first, both on NQ with ES as the transfer: **(1) an NY-open sweep grid that trades overnight, prior-day, London and Venom-box levels in both fade and continuation mode, and (2) the Silver Bullet / 2022-Model engine already in your repo, with placebo hours built into the grid.** Opening-gap fill and FVG-continuation grids are cheap follow-ups. Order blocks, Unicorn, OTE, SMT and weekly profiles should not get grids. The honest prior is that the fade arms will lose. The most likely "positive" result is that the continuation arm rediscovers NQ open momentum under a new name.

## Thirteen named setups collapse into one engine and a clock

ICT (Michael Huddleston) never published a written rulebook. Every mechanical version of his setups is a community or vendor interpretation, mostly from LuxAlgo's concept library, TradingView scripts and Quantum Algo guides. Judged by how much content, how many scripts and how many tag pages each one has, the **Silver Bullet and the 2022 Mentorship Model are clearly the most prominent setups**. Next come the Judas Swing / Power of Three, the Unicorn, OTE, Turtle Soup, the opening-gap / first-presented-FVG concepts, and the newer 2025 Venom model ([TV silverbullet scripts](https://id.tradingview.com/scripts/silverbullet/); [TV ict2022mentorship](https://in.tradingview.com/scripts/ict2022mentorship/); [TV Unicorn guide](https://www.tradingview.com/chart/XAUUSD/zb6bDQzM-The-Unicorn-Model-Guide-to-ICT-s-Best-Standalone-setup); [TV ictvenom ideas](https://www.tradingview.com/ideas/ictvenom)). No source measures popularity directly, so this ranking is a judgement from content volume.

Stripped of vocabulary, nearly all of them share one sequence. Price sweeps a pool of liquidity (it trades through a prior high or low and closes back inside). Displacement then breaks short-term structure (the MSS). That leg leaves a three-candle fair value gap, and a limit order rests in the gap. The stop goes beyond the sweep wick and the target is the opposite pool. The **2022 Model is that sequence with no clock** ([Quantum Algo](https://www.quantum-algo.com/blog/guides/ict-2022-model-complete-guide/); [LuxAlgo Model 2022](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/model-2022/)). The **Silver Bullet is the same sequence confined to 03:00–04:00, 10:00–11:00 or 14:00–15:00 ET**, with the FVG required to form inside the window ([LuxAlgo Silver Bullet](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/silver-bullet.md)). The Judas Swing and Power of Three anchor the sweep to the midnight open and the Asian range, and LuxAlgo calls PO3 "descriptive, not mechanical" ([LuxAlgo Judas Swing](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/judas-swing.md); [LuxAlgo AMD](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/accumulation-manipulation-distribution/)). **Venom** anchors it to the 08:00–09:30 ET "box" and looks for a sweep of one side after the 09:30 open. NQ is called its cleanest market, and ICT has not released full exit rules ([TradingFinder Venom script](https://cn.tradingview.com/script/0LMNMrpu-ICT-Venom-Trading-Model-TradingFinder-SMC-NY-Session-2025Setup); [TV Venom idea](https://cn.tradingview.com/chart/NQM2025/Gv0GpBle-Understanding-the-ICT-Venom-Model)). ICT's **Turtle Soup** relabels Connors and Raschke's 1995 failed-breakout fade. In its original form that is a daily-bar trade held 2–6 days, so only an intraday proxy fits your no-overnight rule ([LuxAlgo Turtle Soup](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/turtle-soup.md); [quantvps](https://www.quantvps.com/blog/turtle-soup-trading-strategy)).

The remaining setups are geometric overlays on that engine, not separate signals. The **Unicorn** is a breaker block overlapping an FVG ([TV Unicorn guide](https://www.tradingview.com/chart/XAUUSD/zb6bDQzM-The-Unicorn-Model-Guide-to-ICT-s-Best-Standalone-setup)). **OTE** is a 62–79% retracement of the displacement leg with 70.5% as the sweet spot, and LuxAlgo itself says it "is not a standalone signal" ([LuxAlgo OTE](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/optimal-trade-entry/)). The **opening range gap** (prior RTH close to the 09:30 open) and its 50% "consequent encroachment" level are the one family with a distinct mechanism ([LuxAlgo Opening Gap](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/opening-gap.md)). So is the **first presented FVG**, the first 1-minute gap after 09:30 ([TV ICT 9:30am First FVG](https://tradingview.com/script/K587gQPT-ICT-9-30am-First-FVG)).

| Setup | Window (ET) | Mechanically objective | Discretionary in ICT teaching | Fits no-overnight rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Silver Bullet | 03–04, 10–11, 14–15 | window, FVG geometry, entry, stop | which pool is the "draw", whether the sweep counts | Yes |
| 2022 Model | killzones (08:30–11, 13:30–16) | sweep → MSS → FVG order | displacement strength, HTF bias, which FVG | Yes |
| Judas / PO3 | 00–05, or NY variant 09:30–10:30 | midnight open, sweep, reclaim | whether today "should" reverse | Yes |
| Venom | box 08:00–09:30, trade 09:30–~11 | box, sweep, FVG | targets and exits unreleased | Yes |
| Opening range gap / 1st FVG | 09:30–10:00 | gap, CE, first gap | context | Yes |
| Turtle Soup (ICT intraday) | any killzone | level, reclaim, stop | which level | Yes |
| Turtle Soup (Raschke original) | daily | fully mechanical | none | **No (2–6 day hold)** |
| Unicorn, OTE, order blocks | overlays | zone math | swing and block definitions | Yes |

Even the clocks are not settled. Killzone boundaries differ by **30 to 90 minutes across ICT sources**: NY open is 07:00–10:00 in one source and 08:30–11:00 for index futures in another. The popular `smartmoneyconcepts` Python package hard-codes killzones in UTC, so they drift an hour against New York time each winter ([LuxAlgo Killzones](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/killzones/); [smc.py](https://github.com/joshyattridge/smart-money-concepts/blob/master/smartmoneyconcepts/smc.py)). Displacement has **no canonical numeric threshold** anywhere in the sources ([LuxAlgo Displacement](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/displacement/)). Each such definitional choice is a hidden trial, and it has to be fixed before the run.

## Baseline-controlled tests erase every ICT edge except the sweep

The evidence splits cleanly. Tests that compare ICT signals to a placebo, a matched-return null or random entry, and that charge costs, come out uniformly negative. Favourable numbers come almost entirely from vendor pages, small samples, in-sample optimisation or cost-free runs. A placebo-controlled FVG study compared real gaps across crypto, FX and equity-index futures with placebo zones of identical size and distance. It found a **pooled real-minus-placebo difference of −0.04 percentage points (95% CI about −1.2 to +1.1 pp)**. In that study a 54% raw hit rate looks "significant" against 50% but not against the placebo ([TradingView placebo FVG](https://de.tradingview.com/script/Kbq3WncE-Fair-Value-Gap-FVG-Statistics-with-Placebo-Control/)). A million-bar random-walk simulation shows that FVG "fill rates" of 73–83% are exactly what random levels produce ([ThorTradeCopier](https://thortradecopier.com/blog/does-ict-smart-money-concepts-work)). The 2026 SSRN paper testing 44 formalised SMC detectors on S&P 500 daily bars found 38 that look significant against a zero-return null, but **none beat random entry under a matched-return null with Benjamini-Hochberg correction** ([SSRN 7483658](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7483658)). On EURUSD 1-minute data from 2019–2025, **0 of 54 ICT encodings were profitable after a 0.5-pip cost, and the best profit factor was 0.89** ([IndicatorEdge](https://indicatoredge.io/smart-money-research)). StatOasis ran 648 frictionless daily-bar tests on SPY, QQQ, DIA and IWM. Only 1 of 32 t-stats reached 2, and no variant beat buy-and-hold ([StatOasis](https://statoasis.com/overfit/research/ict-backtest-what-survives)).

The study closest to your setup is a YouTube backtest of **25,000 random Silver Bullet configurations on 16 years of NQ and ES 1-minute data**, with the last two years held out and costs included. Only **420 configs (1.7%) survived** the funnel of profitability, luck correction and an ES cold run. Three of its findings matter for your grids. **Requiring a liquidity sweep was the only rule that consistently added edge.** The 15-minute bias and MSS filters *hurt*. **The ICT hours did no better than other hours, and the famous 10:00 window was slightly negative** ([Gistly summary](https://gist.ly/youtube-summarizer/i-backtested-25000-ict-silver-bullet-trades)). Treat the study with caution. Its fills were optimistic touch-fills, the holdout was a strong bull market that flatters dip-buying after sweeps, the cost amounts were not stated, and only a summary was verifiable. A second independent tester ran the Silver Bullet rules across all 23 hours on 1-minute ES, NQ, YM and GC data from 2011–2026, with 2 ticks of slippage and $4.50 round-trip commission. They concluded that "**the Silver Bullet hours are not special, and the model is not a profitable strategy on its own**." They attributed any apparent edge to higher volatility, which makes fixed costs a smaller share of a wider stop ([TV Silver Bullet GBB](https://br.tradingview.com/script/teL2dDvS-Silver-Bullet-GBB/)).

The direction of the sweep effect is the real open question. Mahadzva's walk-forward study of six ICT rules on intraday G10 FX found "no evidence that ICT/SMC concepts work as literally taught." In that study three independently built sweep rules agreed in **65 of 66 folds that a swept level predicts continuation, not reversal**. Its one survivor was a CHoCH+FVG signal traded *in the opposite direction*, and it failed a Deflated Sharpe check ([SSRN 7430998](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7430998); [ThorTradeCopier summary](https://thortradecopier.com/blog/does-ict-smart-money-concepts-work)). The microstructure literature explains why both readings exist. Osler's dealer-order data shows that take-profit orders cluster *at* round numbers, which produces small bounces. Stop-losses cluster *just beyond* them, which produces cascades once the level breaks ([NY Fed SR125](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr125.pdf); [NY Fed SR150](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/staff_reports/sr150.html)). The bounce excess at published support and resistance levels was only **60.8% vs 56.2% at arbitrary levels**, a 4.6-point edge in 1990s FX that was never tested for profitability ([NY Fed EPR](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/epr/00v06n2/0007osle.pdf)). So the mechanism ICT invokes is real but small. It argues at least as strongly for continuation through a level as for ICT's "sweep and reverse." The intraday-timing effect with peer-reviewed support in index futures is momentum into the close, not a killzone reversal ([Gao et al., JFE 2018](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf); [Baltussen et al.](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/EOD.pdf)).

Huddleston adds no counterweight. There is **no verified, broker-statement-backed public track record**. The frequently cited contest episode is the 2024 Robbins World Cup entry, and claims about its outcome rest on unverified forum posts ([NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/showthread.php?p=899824); [Writofinance](https://www.writofinance.com/inner-circle-trader-ict-michael/)). The high "prior-day high gets hit" and "gap fills" statistics sold by stats sites (81–88% for prior-day extremes, ~76% for ES gap fills) are largely mechanical: daily range compared with distance to the level. They measure hit rates, not conditional returns ([Edgeful](https://www.edgeful.com/blog/posts/inside-day-in-trading-breakout-data); [mypivots](https://mypivots.com/gapfill)).

## Your funnel has already tested most ICT mechanisms

Five of your existing families overlap ICT ideas, and their results set the priors. The only open-session edge that survived your holdout is **NQ noise-band, a continuation trade**. NQ v2 finalists had a median Sharpe of 1.58 and beat 98% of random-entry runs, and #1 made +10.3% on the 2025+ holdout. ES failed on the holdout (Sharpe −0.20), and RTY and ZN had 0% profitable versions ([NQ noise_band_v2](../futures-results/notes/NQ/noise_band_v2.html); [ES holdout](../futures-results/data/ES/noise_band_portfolio_v4_holdout.json); commits e84559a, e1aeaf5). Fading extremes has failed every time. **Drift-burst had 0 survivors from 10,944 ES configs, and the FX-fix reversal (ICT's "London close") had 0 of 405 profitable on 6E**, best PF 0.96 (commits d7cd024, 05d9b26). ES mean reversion had 0 of 7 finalists pass validation, and NQ mean reversion beat only 53% of random runs ([ES mean_reversion_v6](../futures-results/notes/ES/mean_reversion_v6__15m.html); [NQ mean_reversion_v7](../futures-results/notes/NQ/mean_reversion_v7__15m.html)). The intraday ES–NQ spread, the closest analogue to ICT's "SMT divergence", had 0 of 6 pass validation (commit 7eae686).

The repo also already contains a **complete, causal ICT Silver Bullet implementation**. It is `src/strategies/ict_silver_bullet/`, with feature modules for swings, FVGs, sweeps, liquidity, market structure and displacement, each carrying explicit `availability_ts`/`confirmation_ts`. A 400-config SPY pilot was run in August 2026. Of the 182 configs that completed, the **median profit factor was 1.005 and the median Sharpe 0.06**, statistically indistinguishable from noise. The NY AM leg was profitable in only 42% of configs, against 58% for the PM leg ([spy_pilot_400 results](../channel-breakout-backtest/runs/results/experiment_id=spy_pilot_400/); [signals.py](../last-half-hour-backtest/src/strategies/ict_silver_bullet/signals.py)). That pilot had no data split, used equity costs, had no London window and had an unknown trial count, so it is weak evidence. It agrees with the external studies that the 10:00 window is the worst one.

## Ranked test plan: two NQ grids first, four optional follow-ups

The ranking weighs three things: prior plausibility from the evidence above, how much a result would teach you given your existing families, and how much code already exists. All grids share fixed rules. Bars are 1-minute. Signals fire on the bar close and fill at the next open, or a limit order rests from the bar after confirmation. Each session takes at most one trade, and positions are flat by 16:00 ET or the stated window end. Contracts are whole and sized by notional, as in your other futures families. Stops are live intrabar, and same-bar ambiguity resolves `stop_first`. Every swing, FVG and level must be filtered on its availability timestamp.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | NY-open sweep: fade vs continuation (Judas / Venom / NY-open reversal) | 432 | NQ dev → ES, YM transfer | Tests the one ICT element with support, settles the contested direction, and benchmarks directly against your only surviving edge | Fade arm loses; continuation arm may echo noise-band |
| 2 | Silver Bullet / 2022 Model engine with placebo windows | 432 | NQ → ES | Most popular setups, code already exists, built-in placebo answers the "special hours" claim | Null; two independent tests already negative |
| 3 | Opening range gap fill / CE | 162 | ES, NQ | Distinct mechanism, wide targets keep costs small relative to R, tests the "70% half-fill in 30 min" claim | Weak; gap-fill rates are mostly mechanical |
| 4 | FVG continuation after displacement | 216 | NQ → ES | Untested continuation counterpart of drift-burst; isolates the FVG with no ICT sequence | Near-null; placebo study says FVG ≈ random |
| 5 | Prior-day high/low turtle soup, all RTH | 216 | ES, NQ, YM | Cheap negative control that overlaps mean reversion and drift-burst | Fails on costs |
| 6 | London / Asian range raid | 324 | 6E → GC | Only grid that tests the FX-origin killzones; needs a decision that 02:00–11:00 ET counts as intraday | Low; FX-fix and Mahadzva both point against fades |

### 1. NY-open sweep, fade vs continuation (432 configs)

Between 09:30 and 09:30 + `window`, price trades beyond `level` and then closes back inside within the confirmation rule. **Fade mode** trades the reclaim, which is the Judas/Venom reading. **Continuation mode** trades the same penetration in the break direction, the Mahadzva/Osler reading. The continuation arm is the essential control. If fade loses and continuation wins, the "sweep" framing adds nothing beyond the NQ open momentum noise-band already captures. Venom's box replaces the Asian range from the original design. Its 08:00–09:30 definition comes from the sources above, and the Asian range is nested inside the overnight range anyway.

| Axis | Levels | n |
|---|---|---|
| `level` | Overnight H/L (18:00–09:30) · prior-RTH H/L · London H/L (02:00–05:00) · Venom box H/L (08:00–09:30) | 4 |
| `window` (minutes after 09:30) | 15 · 30 · 60 | 3 |
| `confirm` | reclaim close within 1 bar · within 3 bars · reclaim + MSS (close beyond last available 3/3 swing) | 3 |
| `mode` | fade · continuation | 2 |
| `stop` | sweep extreme + 2 ticks · 1.0 × ATR(14, 1m) from entry | 2 |
| `exit` | 2R · opposite side of the same range · time exit 12:00 ET | 3 |

Run NQ on 2018–22 dev. Transfer to ES as the strict test, because ES failed both noise-band and last-half-hour. YM is a third check, since 58% of its noise-band versions were profitable. On finalists, add a **placebo-level benchmark** that runs the same rules on synthetic levels at equal distance from the 09:30 open. This is the futures analogue of Osler's 10,000 arbitrary levels and the placebo-FVG control. Without it, a result only measures open-time volatility. `liquidity.py` currently has no overnight levels because it was written for RTH-only FMP data, so it needs extending ([liquidity.py](../last-half-hour-backtest/src/features/liquidity.py)).

### 2. Silver Bullet / 2022 Model with placebo windows (432 configs)

Reuse `ict_silver_bullet`. The window axis carries the experiment. It holds the three canonical hours, a "2022 Model" level that accepts any setup in the 08:30–11:00 or 13:30–16:00 killzones, and two placebo hours. The sweep toggle tests whether "liquidity" adds anything to a plain FVG, which the 25k-config study says it does.

| Axis | Levels | n |
|---|---|---|
| `window` | 10:00–11:00 · 14:00–15:00 · 03:00–04:00 · 2022-Model killzones · placebo 11:00–12:00 · placebo 12:30–13:30 | 6 |
| `fvg_min` | 0 · 0.5 × ATR · 1.0 × ATR (ATR at confirmation) | 3 |
| `entry` | FVG proximal edge · CE (midpoint) | 2 |
| `stop` | FVG far edge · swept extreme | 2 |
| `target` | 2R · nearest opposing available liquidity · end of window | 3 |
| `require_sweep` | yes · no | 2 |

Fix these instead of gridding them: swing 5/5, sweep reclaim within 3 bars, MSS off (the external study found it hurts), displacement true range ≥ 1.5 × ATR on the FVG middle bar, FVG max age 30 bars, and the limit order must fill inside the window. The pilot's known bugs need fixing first: the `ny_pm` Polars schema error that crashed 38 configs, the hard block on the London window, and equity sizing and costs. Expect thin trade counts. The external study put textbook frequency at about **34 trades per year**, which is below your funnel's minimums for any single window.

### 3. Opening range gap fill (162 configs)

If the absolute gap between the 09:30 open and the prior 16:00 ET close exceeds `gap_min`, trade toward or away from the gap's CE. Axes: `gap_min` 0.1 · 0.25 · 0.5 × daily ATR (3); `entry` at the 09:30 open · after the first 5-minute bar if CE is not yet reached · on the first 1-minute FVG pointing toward the gap (3); `target` CE · full fill · 10:00 time exit (3); `stop` 0.5 × gap · 1 × gap beyond the open · first 5-minute extreme (3); `mode` fade · continuation (2). Wide gap targets make it the grid least exposed to the cost wall. It runs against noise-band's open momentum on NQ, so ES is the natural development contract here. The "70% half-fill within 30 minutes" statistic attributed to ICT is unverified ([TV MW ICT Open Range Gap](https://th.tradingview.com/script/q8eZ6kK4/)).

### 4–6. Follow-ups worth running only if compute is idle

The **FVG continuation grid** (216) trades the first retrace into a 1-minute FVG left by a displacement bar, 09:45–15:30. Axes: displacement 1.5 · 2.0 · 3.0 × ATR (3); entry proximal · CE (2); max wait 5 · 15 · 30 bars (3); stop at the FVG far edge · the bar-1 extreme (2); exit 1.5R · 3R · 30-minute time exit (3); VWAP trend gate off · on (2). Benchmark it with placebo zones as in the TradingView study. Together with drift-burst it brackets the "extreme 1-minute move" question.

The **turtle-soup grid** (216) fades reclaims of the prior day's or prior week's high or low all session. Axes: level PDH/PDL · prior-week H/L · both (3); penetration any · ≥ 4 ticks · ≥ 0.1 × daily ATR (3); reclaim within 1 · 5 bars (2); entry market · limit on retest (2); stop extreme + 2 ticks · 2 × penetration (2); exit 2R · VWAP · 16:00 (3). Run it as a negative control.

The **London raid grid** (324) on 6E then GC is the only test of ICT's FX-origin killzones. Axes: range Asia 19:00–00:00 · Asia+early 19:00–02:00 · prior NY 08:00–17:00 (3); raid window 02:00–05:00 · 08:00–11:00 · both (3); confirm reclaim in 1 bar · in 3 bars · reclaim + FVG (3); stop raid extreme + 2 ticks · 1 × ATR (2); exit range midpoint · opposite edge · 11:00 time exit (3); minimum penetration 0 · 0.25 × range height (2). It requires accepting 02:00–11:00 ET trades as intraday. Your FX-fix family used an 08:00–17:00 envelope.

Skip RTY, ZN, ZB, CL and 6J for all of these grids. ICT sources rarely mention them, applying the models there is untested extrapolation, and noise-band found nothing on RTY or ZN.

### What not to grid

**Order blocks, breaker/mitigation blocks and the Unicorn** are the most ambiguously defined ICT objects. Popular code picks the lowest-low candle between swing and break rather than ICT's "last opposite-closing candle". smc.py's version is non-causal, and an isolated FX test found order-block bounces fail 57% of the time ([LuxAlgo order block](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/bullish-bearish-order-block/); [FXNX](https://fxnx.com/en/blog/smart-money-concepts-work-backtest-evidence)). **OTE** reduces to Fibonacci zones, which an academic study found no better than random zones ([IndicatorEdge citing Tsinaslanidis et al.](https://indicatoredge.io/smart-money-research)). **SMT divergence** maps onto your failed ES–NQ spread family. **Daily bias, premium/discount and weekly profiles** have no published deterministic definition and are hindsight-labelled. HTF bias and MSS filters hurt in the one large ES/NQ test. Add mechanical bias proxies (prior-day close in the upper or lower half, price vs the midnight open) only as a second-stage ablation on a grid that already has finalists, not as standalone families.

## Five build rules decide whether an ICT test means anything

**First, do not import the `smartmoneyconcepts` package.** It stamps FVGs on the middle bar with one bar of look-ahead. It confirms swings with a centred ±50-bar window. It writes BOS/CHoCH to earlier swings and then deletes unbroken ones retroactively. It sets liquidity tolerance from the *whole dataset's* high-low range. Only its previous-high/low and session functions are causal ([smc.py](https://github.com/joshyattridge/smart-money-concepts/blob/master/smartmoneyconcepts/smc.py)). Your own `src/features/` layer already handles this correctly ([swings.py](../last-half-hour-backtest/src/features/swings.py); [fvg.py](../last-half-hour-backtest/src/features/fvg.py); [sweeps.py](../last-half-hour-backtest/src/features/sweeps.py)).

**Second, set a minimum stop distance that respects the cost wall.** Your round-trip cost is about **0.6 ES points or 0.75 NQ points** (2 ticks plus $5). A textbook ICT stop of 3–5 ES points pays 12–20% of R in costs, and a 2-point ES stop pays 30%. Mahadzva found exactly this: one strategy's per-trade edge was smaller than commission until a minimum stop was imposed. Use contract-specific floors (for example ≥ 4 ES points, ≥ 8 NQ points, or ≥ 0.5 × ATR) rather than a flat 8 ticks. These floors are my arithmetic, not a sourced ICT rule.

**Third, make fills conservative.** The SPY pilot's median limit fill rate of 0.44 shows how much results depend on the fill rule. Require a 1-tick trade-through for limit fills at an FVG edge or CE. Report the share of each config's trades that were intrabar-ambiguous.

**Fourth, handle clocks explicitly.** Compute every window in `America/New_York`, build London windows in London time and convert per date as your FX-fix family does, and flag roll dates where the Asian range straddles Databento's 00:00 UTC roll. Databento `ohlcv-1m` likely omits no-trade minutes, which thins overnight ranges and k-bar reclaim counts. This has not been verified for your data.

**Fifth, count trials honestly.** Each definitional choice fixed before the run counts as a trial: swing length, displacement threshold, killzone variant, FVG size rule. The bar for a data-mined field is a t-stat above 3 ([Harvey, Liu & Zhu](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592)). Your NQ 2023–24 and 2025+ data have already been inspected by noise-band, mean reversion, squeeze, last-half-hour, month-end and convexity, so an NQ holdout result must be labelled "previously inspected." An ES transfer that survives is worth more than an NQ win.

## Conclusion

ICT's real contribution to a systematic researcher is not a strategy but a list of **specific, falsifiable claims about where stops rest and when they get run**, wrapped in vocabulary that makes those claims unfalsifiable in discretionary use. Encoding them mechanically turns that into a clean experiment. The most valuable outcome of grid 1 is not a tradeable fade. It is learning whether swept overnight and prior-day levels on NQ and ES behave like Osler's stop cascades (continuation) or like ICT's liquidity raids (reversal). Either answer sharpens your existing NQ open-momentum edge. A continuation result might give that edge a better trigger, and a fade result would mark where to stand aside.

Set expectations before running. Two independent 1-minute index-futures tests of the Silver Bullet already came back null or near-null after costs, and every baseline-controlled FVG and order-block test came back at zero. Your own fade families failed the same cost wall. A null across grids 1 and 2 is therefore the most likely outcome, and it is still a useful, publishable-quality result for your funnel. Treat any surviving fade config as suspect until it beats placebo levels and placebo hours, transfers to ES, and clears a deflated Sharpe with the full definitional trial count.
