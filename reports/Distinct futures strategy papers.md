# Fix and Settlement Flows Lead the Queue

The best new standalone family to put through the ablation suite is the **FX-fix "W-pattern" in 6E and 6J** from Krohn, Mueller and Whelan (*Journal of Finance* 2024). The second is the **3rd-Friday SOQ "tent"** in the equity-index futures from Baltussen, Terstegge and Whelan (2024 working paper). Both trade the clock rather than a price pattern, so neither overlaps the existing families. Both rest on a dealer-hedging or settlement mechanism that a published calendar rule does not obviously erase, and both need nothing beyond the 1-minute bars already on disk. Two more standalone tests are worth one honest run each. The first is a **volume-gated fade of extreme 5-minute "drift bursts"** (Christensen, Oomen and Renò), which is futures-native but sits close to the existing mean-reversion family. The second is the **overnight drift with a "buy-the-dip" condition** (Boyarchenko, Larsen and Whelan, *RFS* 2023). It has the strongest published evidence of anything here, but its own authors now report it averaged about zero in 2021-2025, so a run is mostly a decay check. The most valuable work that is not a new strategy is an **overlay**: volatility targeting (Harvey et al. 2018, Wang and Yan 2021, Bongaerts et al. 2020) applied to the strategies that already passed the holdout, plus one or two regime gates (a statistical jump model and the momentum turning-points state). The deep-learning momentum papers, intraday ML forecasters, carry, cross-sectional momentum, VWAP trend and Kalman trend were rejected. Each failed on cost fragility, data the suite does not have, a universe too small, or overlap with families already tested. None of the sources reports a profit factor, so every run has to produce its own PF next to Sharpe, and most report gross rather than net Sharpe.

## Four standalone candidates clear the bar, and two are dealer-flow effects

Each candidate was scored from 1 to 5 on four axes. Evidence strength covers venue, whether the full text was read, and whether the result held net of costs. Post-2018 durability asks whether anything shows the effect alive inside the user's 2018-2026 window. Distinctness is measured against the ten families already tested. Data fit asks whether 1-minute OHLCV on these nine contracts is enough, and whether the trade count gives the 2025-2026 holdout any statistical power. The table ranks the standalone candidates. Overlays and filters are ranked separately further down, because they cannot be judged on standalone P&L.

| Rank | Candidate (paper) | Markets | Evidence | Durability | Distinct | Data fit | Best reported Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | FX-fix W-pattern (Krohn-Mueller-Whelan, JF 2024) | 6E, 6J | 4 | 3 | 5 | 5 | ~1.2-2.0 gross; 0.12-1.03 at 50% of spot spread |
| 2 | 3rd-Friday SOQ tent (Baltussen-Terstegge-Whelan, WP 2024) | ES, NQ, RTY, YM | 3 | 4 | 5 | 4 | not retrieved |
| 3 | Volume-gated drift-burst reversal (Christensen-Oomen-Renò) | all nine | 3 | 2 | 3 | 5 | no trading P&L reported |
| 4 | Overnight drift, buy-the-dip (Boyarchenko-Larsen-Whelan, RFS 2023) | ES, NQ, YM, RTY | 5 | 1 | 5 | 4 | 1.8 gross / 1.1 net (2004-2020) |
| 5 | Bond-to-equity cross-asset TSMOM (Pitkäjärvi-Suominen-Vaittinen, JFE 2020) | ZN to ES/NQ/RTY/YM | 3 | 2 | 4 | 3 | ~45% above plain TSMOM (20 countries) |
| 6 | FOMC and month-end calendar pack (Lucca-Moench; Cieslak et al.; Etula et al.) | ES and peers | 4 | 1 | 5 | 2 | pre-FOMC >1.1 (1994-2011) |
| 7 | Oil-to-equity lag (Driesprong-Jacobsen-Maat, JFE 2008) | CL to ES/RTY | 3 | 2 | 4 | 3 | not retrieved |

The ranking rewards durability and data fit over raw evidence. The overnight drift has the best paper, but its authors' July 2026 post finds the 2:00-3:00 a.m. ET window **averaged close to zero from January 2021 to December 2025**. Their explanation is that the standard deviation of end-of-day order imbalance fell from 6.5% to 2.9% ([Liberty Street 2026](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/07/the-disappearing-overnight-drift/)). The pre-FOMC drift has the same problem: Kurov, Wolfe and Gilbert find it **essentially disappeared after 2015** ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v40y2021ics1544612320315956.html)). The two flow effects ranked first and second have no such published obituary. Each also gives a natural negative control: the yen's Europe leg for the fix, and non-expiry Fridays for the SOQ.

## The FX-fix W-pattern is the strongest standalone test

Krohn, Mueller and Whelan show that **the dollar appreciates into the Tokyo, ECB and London fixes and depreciates after them**. Around the clock this gives a W-shaped return path, which they attribute to dealers pre-hedging predictable USD demand at the fixes ([Bank of Canada SWP 2021-48](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/swp2021-48.pdf)). The effect is large before costs. Going long USD before the Tokyo fix and reversing after it earned **12.0% a year in JPY and 10.44% in EUR**. A dollar run through the EUR Europe leg grew to **$27 from 1999 to 2019** ([BoC WP](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/swp2021-48.pdf)). Costs bite hard. At the full indicative spot spread most windows go negative. At half the spread, the EUR pre-ECB window still earns **8.64%** and the post-London window **5.46%**, with Sharpe ratios of 0.12 to 1.03 across windows ([BoC WP](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/swp2021-48.pdf)). Two findings matter most for the suite. The pattern **also appears in CME FX futures from 2006**. And the **Tokyo leg decayed after about 2013** net of costs, while the London reversal stayed strong ([BoC WP](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/swp2021-48.pdf)). The prior should therefore centre on the 6E Europe legs, with the Tokyo legs as secondary cells.

This ranks first because the trade count gives real power. One round trip per leg per day yields roughly 2,000 trades per leg over the sample and about 450 in the holdout, while the grid stays small. It is also the cleanest available test of whether CME futures spreads, which are tighter than the indicative spot quotes the paper charged, leave the edge intact after 2019. The paper's cost analysis stops in 2019, and no source checked whether futures make the Tokyo leg profitable after that. That gap is exactly what this run would fill.

**Exact rules.** 6E and 6J are quoted in USD per foreign unit, so "long USD" means **short the future**. Define every boundary in the fix's own time zone and convert to ET with a tz database. A hard-coded ET clock drifts by an hour in the weeks when US and European daylight saving time disagree. The paper's ET windows ([BoC WP](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/swp2021-48.pdf)) map onto the futures session as follows.

| Leg | Window (ET, US summer time) | Fix anchor | Position | Expected prior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tokyo pre-fix | 18:00 (after the Globex halt; paper starts at 17:00) to 20:55 (19:55 in US winter) | Tokyo 9:55 JST | short 6E / 6J | decayed after ~2013 |
| Tokyo post-fix | 20:55 to 02:00 | — | long 6E / 6J | decayed after ~2013 |
| Pre-ECB | 02:00 to 08:15 | ECB 14:15 CET | short 6E | strongest net leg |
| ECB-to-London | 08:15 to 11:00 | — | flat | no clear sign in the paper |
| Post-London | 11:00 to 17:00 | London 16:00 (WM/R) | long 6E | robust net leg |

Enter at the open of the first 1-minute bar at or after the boundary and exit at the open of the bar at the next boundary. The yen **appreciates in Asian hours and moves the other way after London**. Its Europe leg lost about 6% in total over the paper's sample ([BoC WP](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/swp2021-48.pdf)), so the 6J Europe leg is a built-in **negative control**. If that leg comes out profitable in validation, the engine has a sign or timing bug. Breedon and Ranaldo's finding that currencies depreciate during their own local trading hours ([SNB WP](https://www.snb.ch/en/mmr/papers/id/working_paper_2011_04)) describes the same flows. It belongs in this grid as a window variant, not as a separate family.

**Ablation grid (about 160 cells, plus a reporting-only cost axis).** The paper finds reversal returns are higher when volatility is high and can be predicted with the VIX ([BoC WP](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/swp2021-48.pdf)). That supports a realized-volatility gate built from the bars.

| Axis | Values |
|---|---|
| Leg set | pre-ECB only; post-London only; both Europe legs; Tokyo pair only; all four |
| Boundary offset around each fix | −30, −15, 0, +15 min |
| Tokyo leg start (Tokyo sets only) | 18:00, 18:30 ET |
| Contract | 6E, 6J |
| Volatility gate | none; trailing 20-day realized vol from 5-min bars above its 1-year median |
| Cost (report, do not select on) | 1 tick per side, 2 ticks per side, plus commission |

Pick the configuration on 2023-2024 validation using PF and Sharpe at **2 ticks per side**, then open the holdout once. Report each leg's PF on its own, because a both-legs cell can hide one leg that loses money.

## The 3rd-Friday SOQ tent is mechanical and rarely traded

Baltussen, Terstegge and Whelan document a **"tent"** in 24-hour index futures. Prices rise from the regular close on the 3rd Thursday, **peak exactly at the Special Opening Quotation on 3rd-Friday morning**, and then reverse. The pattern appears **only for AM/SOQ-settled contracts**, not PM-settled ones. The authors estimate a wealth transfer of about **$3.5bn a year** in SPX options and propose market-maker charm hedging as the mechanism ([AEA 2025 program](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2025/program/paper/N7rsBN2N); [AFA listing](https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=98196)). It ranks second for three reasons. Its cause is the design of settlement, not a calendar rule that arbitrageurs can front-run cheaply. The AM/PM split gives a built-in placebo. And it needs only a list of 3rd Fridays adjusted for exchange holidays. The weakness is evidence depth. This is a working paper, the research did not retrieve its Sharpe ratios or t-statistics, and the event count is small. From January 2018 to October 2026 there are about **106 monthly expiries**, and only about 22 fall in the 2025-2026 holdout. The rise of short-dated SPX options since 2022 could change the size of charm hedging into the monthly AM settlement. That is my inference, not a finding in the paper, and it is the main durability risk.

**Exact rules.** Monthly SPX options settle AM on every 3rd Friday, so test all monthly 3rd Fridays, not only the quarterly futures expiries. Go long the front ES contract at the close of the 3rd-Thursday regular session (16:00 ET bar). Exit at the 09:30 ET bar on Friday, which approximates the SOQ because the SOQ is built from constituents' opening prints. The optional reversal leg goes short from the Friday exit to the Friday 16:00 close. Run the identical rule on the Thursday-Friday pair one week earlier and one week later as the **placebo**. A real effect should beat the placebos and most randomly shifted Fridays.

| Axis | Values |
|---|---|
| Entry | Thu 15:00, Thu 16:00, Thu 18:00 (Globex reopen), Fri 02:00 ET |
| Exit | Fri 09:25, 09:30, 09:35, 10:00 ET |
| Expiry set | quarterly only, all monthly |
| Reversal leg | off; short to Fri 16:00; short to Mon 16:00 |
| Contract | ES, NQ, RTY, YM (and an equal-weight basket) |
| Control | real 3rd Friday; placebo week −1; placebo week +1 |

This gives 4 × 4 × 2 × 3 = 96 rule cells per contract, before the controls. With so few events, select on 2018-2024 combined and report bootstrap confidence intervals on mean return per event alongside PF and Sharpe. A holdout of about 22 trades can reject a large effect, but it cannot confirm a small one.

## Burst reversal and the overnight drift each earn one run

### Volume-gated drift-burst fade: futures-native, but adjacent to mean reversion

Christensen, Oomen and Renò detect **"drift bursts"**: points where a kernel-smoothed local drift is extreme relative to local spot volatility. They use tick data on six asset classes including **ES, the 10-year note, 6E and CL** ([arXiv 2601.08974](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08974)). At a critical value of 4.5 there are **202 bursts in ES, roughly one a week**. Roughly **71% of events reverse**, and the per-contract reversal rate "rarely drops below 65%". Regressing the next 5-minute return on the burst return gives a slope of **−0.23**, with R² around 30% ([arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08974)). The reversal is asymmetric and depends on volume. Negative bursts average **−37.39 bps followed by +8.01 bps**, while positive bursts average +36.41 bps followed by only −5.44 bps. In the double sort, the most-negative, high-volume quartile reverses by **+42.8 bps, against 14.3 bps when volume is low** ([arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08974)). The pattern fits Campbell, Grossman and Wang's prediction that returns after heavy volume reverse more ([Harvard DASH](https://dash.harvard.edu/bitstream/1/3128710/2/campbell_trading.pdf)). Xiao, Yin and Zhao report the same split from the news angle. In equity-index ETFs, **jumps with no news reverse strongly for at least 60 minutes**, and negative jumps reverse more than positive ones, while **jumps on unscheduled news show momentum** ([vLex abstract](https://law-journals-books.vlex.com/vid/jumps-news-and-subsequent-855590156)).

There are three reasons for caution. The paper reports **no trading strategy, no costs and no out-of-sample test**, and it measures returns on mid-quotes ([arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08974)). The sample ends in 2017. And the authors call the ordinary 5-minute reversal "present, but extremely weak": the edge sits only in the extreme tail. On distinctness it scores 3, not 5. The existing mean-reversion family fades price z-scores against an SMA on 5-minute to 1-hour bars, so a burst fade is the same direction of bet. What is new is the trigger, a rare and volatility-normalized shock lasting minutes, gated by volume adjusted for time of day. Treat it as a separate family, and check its daily P&L correlation with the mean-reversion finalists before adding it to the book.

**Exact rules (simplified detector).** On 1-minute bars, let r_k be the k-minute log return ending at bar t. Let σ_loc be the root-mean-square 1-minute return over the prior 5k minutes, excluding the burst window. The statistic is T = r_k / (σ_loc·√k). An event fires when |T| > c, and entry is at the next bar's open after |T| stops rising (a local extremum). Volume gate: the k-minute volume divided by the trailing 20-day mean volume for the same minutes of the day must sit above quantile q. Fade the burst, exit after H minutes, and allow at most one event per instrument per day, as the paper does.

| Axis | Values (paper anchor marked) |
|---|---|
| Burst window k | 3, 5 (paper), 10 min |
| Threshold c | 3.5, 4.0 (paper), 4.5 (paper), 5.0 |
| Volume gate | none, > median, > Q3 (paper), > P90 |
| Side | both; long after drops only (asymmetry) |
| Hold H | 5 (paper), 15, 30 min |
| Session | RTH only, 24h |
| Scheduled-release exclusion | off; skip events within ±5 min of 08:30 and 10:00 ET and ±10 min of 14:00 ET on FOMC days |

That is 1,152 cells per contract. Pool the nine contracts for selection, since ES alone yields only about 50 events a year at c = 4.5. Model slippage as at least one tick on entry, because fills come right after violent bars.

### Overnight drift with a buy-the-dip condition: run it as a decay test

Over 2004-2020, a long ES position held only from 2:00 to 3:00 a.m. ET (the European open) had a gross Sharpe of **1.1**. Widening the window to 1:30-3:30 gave **1.3**. The buy-the-dip version holds 1:30-3:30 only after a day whose last-hour signed volume was negative, and it reached **1.8 gross and 1.1 after paying the spread**. The plain window fell to **−0.5 net** ([NY Fed SR917](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr917.pdf)). Bondarenko and Muravyev independently find that about four hours around the European open account for the entire average return on ES ([Cambridge abstract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/market-return-around-the-clock-a-puzzle/089E33AC0B4D3B9A02CBA31EDF6505B3)). The decay evidence covers the user's whole validation window and half the holdout ([Liberty Street 2026](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/07/the-disappearing-overnight-drift/)). A 2023-2024 validation failure should therefore close the file without a second round.

**Exact rules and grid (36 cells per contract).** The signal is a bar-level proxy for relative signed volume over 15:15-16:15 ET: RSV = Σ sign(close − open)·volume / Σ volume. This proxy is a substitution, because the paper uses signed trades. Go long at t1 and exit at t2 when the condition holds. The axes are t1 ∈ {01:00, 01:30, 02:00}, t2 ∈ {03:00, 03:30, 04:00}, and condition ∈ {none, RSV < 0, RSV in the bottom quintile of the trailing year, prior RTH return < 0}. Run it on ES, NQ, YM and RTY; the authors report the pattern also in NQ and YM ([Liberty Street 2026](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/07/the-disappearing-overnight-drift/)). Report development (2018-2020) and 2021-2022 separately, since the published break falls between them.

## Volatility targeting and regime gates belong on top of existing strategies

The overlays are ranked by expected value added to strategies already in the book, not by standalone P&L.

| Rank | Overlay or filter | Host | Evidence | Durability | Data fit | What it should buy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Volatility targeting, downside and conditional variants | all holdout survivors; long ES/NQ benchmark | 4 | 3 | 5 | lower drawdown and left tail; small Sharpe gain on equity hosts only |
| 2 | Turning-points state gate (Goulding-Harvey-Mazzoleni) | channel/MA/noise-band vs mean reversion | 4 | 3 | 3 | avoid trend trades during slow/fast disagreement |
| 3 | Statistical jump model risk-on/off gate (Shu-Yu-Mulvey) | equity-index hosts | 3 | 3 | 2 | drawdown cut; regimes lag turns by ~2 weeks |
| 4 | Opening-window volume gate (Gao et al.; Baltussen et al.) | noise-band intraday momentum | 3 | 2 | 5 | cheap extra axis; stronger signal on heavy-volume days |
| 5 | Bar microstructure features (BVC-VPIN, Amihud, Kyle λ) | momentum vs MR switch | 2 | 2 | 3 | low; predictive power tracks realized vol |

**Volatility targeting is the highest-value first experiment** because it adds no new alpha risk. Harvey et al. size S&P 500 and 10-year futures with **5-minute data from 09:15 to 14:00 Chicago time**, using an exponentially weighted average of daily realized variance. Scaling raised the US equity Sharpe from **0.40 to 0.48-0.51**, with little sensitivity to the half-life. Intraday data slightly improved Sharpe, vol of vol and the left tail for S&P futures. For bonds, currencies and commodities the Sharpe effect was **negligible**, but volatility of volatility and the tails still fell ([Harvey et al.](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf)). Wang and Yan scale by downside volatility, the square root of the sum of squared negative returns, and beat total-volatility scaling mainly because **downside volatility predicts lower future returns**. They also find that **a fixed 50/50 blend of managed and unmanaged beats estimated "optimal" weights** in real time ([Wang & Yan](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/Downside.pdf)). The case against is serious. Across 103 equity strategies, Cederburg et al. find real-time volatility-managed versions generally earn **lower** Sharpe ratios ([COWY PDF](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf)). Barroso and Detzel find only the **market** portfolio survives costs ([EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:140:y:2021:i:3:p:744-767)). Bongaerts et al. answer with a **conditional** version that changes exposure only in extreme high- or low-volatility states. It improved Sharpe in every market they tested, against 8 of 10 for conventional targeting ([CFA Institute](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2020/conditional-volatility-targeting)). For the intraday strategies, which are flat by the close, the overlay just sets each day's contract count from the forecast σ̂ at the prior close, so it adds no rebalancing cost.

The overlay rule is: contracts_t = round(N₀ × min(L_max, σ_target / σ̂_{t−1})), where σ̂ is the square root of an EWMA of daily realized variance. The grid crosses estimator ∈ {daily close-to-close, 5-min RTH RV, 5-min RTH downside RV}, half-life ∈ {10, 20, 60} days, form ∈ {continuous 1/σ, conditional (scale 0.5× above the trailing-year 80th percentile and 1.5× below the 20th, otherwise 1×)}, cap L_max ∈ {1.5, 2}, and blend ∈ {100% managed, 50/50}. That is 72 cells per host. The conditional thresholds are a proposal, because Bongaerts et al.'s cut-offs are paywalled. Set σ_target so that average exposure in development equals 1×. Then compare against unscaled hosts **at the same average leverage**, not at volatility matched after the fact, because the published critique of Harvey-style scaling turns on that look-ahead. Judge it on maximum drawdown, mean shortfall in the worst 5% of days, PF and Sharpe. Expect better tails everywhere, and a Sharpe gain only on the ES/NQ/RTY/YM hosts.

**The turning-points gate is the cheapest regime filter.** Goulding, Harvey and Mazzoleni classify each period as Bull, Correction, Bear or Rebound from the signs of a slow (12-month) and a fast (1- or 2-month) trailing return. Years with more slow/fast disagreement have lower trend-following Sharpe (**R² = 0.69**), and the count of turning points is almost uncorrelated with volatility (**0.07**). The gate therefore carries information that a volatility filter does not ([Breaking Bad Trends](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P167_Breaking_bad_trends.pdf)). Monthly states need years of history per phase, so translate the lookbacks to daily bars: (slow, fast) ∈ {(252, 21), (126, 21), (60, 10)}. Gate the trend hosts (channel, MA, noise-band) to Bull/Bear and the mean-reversion hosts to Correction/Rebound, against gate-off and an inverse gate. That is 3 lookback pairs × 3 gate modes per host.

**The statistical jump model is the better-documented but data-hungrier gate.** Shu, Yu and Mulvey fit a two-state model on three exponentially weighted features: downside deviation (half-life 10 days) and the Sortino ratio (half-lives 20 and 60 days). It has one tuned jump penalty λ and is held to a one-day delay and 10 bps costs. Over 1990-2023 out of sample it lifted S&P 500 Sharpe from **0.48 to 0.68** and cut maximum drawdown from **−55.2% to −26.6%**, beating an HMM at 0.54 ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.05272v3)). The obstacle is training length. The paper uses a **3,000-day** training window and an 8-year λ validation, while the Databento history gives about 1,250 trading days before the end of development. Use λ ∈ {0, 10, 25, 50, 100, 200} × training ∈ {3y, 5y} × gate ∈ {off, risk-on only, inverse} on equity hosts. Pool ES/NQ/RTY/YM features or train on a longer free daily index history. That last option is my workaround, not the paper's design. Nystrup, Kolm and Lindström report that features built from intraday realized volatility improve state classification ([DTU Orbit](https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/greedy-online-classification-of-persistent-market-states-using-re/)), so add daily RV from the 1-minute bars as a feature variant.

**The volume gate on noise-band is a one-axis add-on.** Gao, Han, Li and Zhou find the first-half-hour return predicts the last half hour more strongly on higher-volume days ([WUSTL](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/market-intraday-momentum/)). Add an axis for first-window volume divided by its trailing 20-day mean for the same minutes, at {none, > 1, > 1.25, > 1.5}, to the existing noise-band grid. The microstructure features rank last. Easley, López de Prado, O'Hara and Zhang show that bar-level VPIN, Amihud and Roll predict the *sign of change* in volatility and autocorrelation with **54-61% accuracy**, but not return direction ([NBER PDF](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f117415.pdf)). Andersen and Bondarenko find that VPIN's predictive power **vanishes once realized volatility is controlled for** ([RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:revfin:v:19:y:2015:i:1:p:1-54.)). Realized volatility is already the input to overlay 1.

## Lower-priority tests and the rejection list

The calendar pack and the two cross-asset signals are cheap to run but have weak post-2018 priors. Lucca and Moench's pre-FOMC drift earned a Sharpe above **1.1** over 1994-2011 from 24 hours of exposure ([NY Fed SR512](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr512.pdf)). It then faded, and a BIS paper finds that after 2011 it **moved to the second day before the meeting and shrank to about 22 bp** ([BIS WP 1079](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1079.pdf)), so include a day −2 entry. Cieslak, Morse and Vissing-Jorgensen find the 1994-2016 equity premium was earned entirely in **even weeks of the FOMC cycle** ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2687614)). No post-2016 out-of-sample test was found. Etula et al.'s month-end "dash for cash" (weak from T−8 to T−4, strong over the next seven days) has old and mixed futures evidence ([OUP](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/33/1/75/5494694); [Atlanta Fed](https://www.atlantafed.org/research/publications/wp/2000/11.aspx)). With about 70 scheduled FOMC meetings in the sample, run all three as one small grid with randomly shifted placebo calendars. For cross-asset, Pitkäjärvi et al. show that **past bond returns predict equity returns positively, and past equity returns predict bond returns negatively** ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v136y2020i1p63-85.html)). Driesprong et al. show that **rising oil predicts lower stock returns** ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v89y2008i2p307-327.html)). Neither paper's exact lookback could be verified, both are daily, and one country with one bond contract strips out the diversification behind the 20-country Sharpe. A grid of ZN or −CL lookback ∈ {5, 21, 63} days × hold ∈ {5, 21} days × target ∈ {ES, RTY} would give an exploratory read only.

| Rejected | Why |
|---|---|
| Deep Momentum Networks (Lim-Zohren-Roberts 2019) | Gross Sharpe 2.80 collapses to **−5.31 at 10 bps** without turnover regularization; a 50-trial hyperparameter search at every refit ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.04912)) |
| Changepoint-detection LSTM (Wood-Roberts-Zohren 2022) | Main results before costs; a 50-futures portfolio Sharpe that a single instrument cannot reproduce ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.13727)) |
| Intraday ML direction forecasting from OHLCV | MNQ 5-minute walk-forward accuracy of 50.0-50.9% against a 51.8% base rate, not significant ([arXiv 2605.17724](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17724)) |

## Running these honestly means counting events and planting controls

The standalone candidates differ by roughly two orders of magnitude in trade count, and the evaluation has to reflect that. The fix legs and the burst fade produce thousands of trades, so the usual dev/validation/holdout funnel and a single holdout opening are enough. The SOQ tent and the calendar pack produce about 22 and about 15 holdout events respectively. For them, the holdout is a check for a large failure, and the decision should lean on bootstrap intervals and placebo-date percentiles over 2018-2024. Every clock-based family needs three engineering details right. Windows must be built in the fix's or exchange's own time zone. The 17:00-18:00 ET Globex halt must be handled. And holidays and early closes must be dropped. Any volume-based gate needs a roll mask. Easley et al. back-adjust prices as a dollar invested and roll volume with them, and Baltussen et al. roll when the back month's volume overtakes the front ([NBER PDF](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f117415.pdf); [ND PDF](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)). A calendar roll would make front-month volume fade before expiry and fire false "low-volume" signals. Finally, costs decide two of the top four. The fix result swings from strongly positive to negative between half and full spot spread, and the overnight drift turns from gross Sharpe 1.1 to −0.5 net in its unconditional form. Report PF and Sharpe at 1 and 2 ticks per side for every cell, and select only on the 2-tick numbers.

## Conclusion

The papers that survive this screen share a mechanism. Their returns come from someone who has to trade at a known time, such as fix-hedging dealers, AM-settlement charm hedgers or month-end cash raisers. They do not come from an indicator reading of past prices. That matters for the book, because these flows have no reason to correlate with the existing breakout, squeeze and mean-reversion families, which all condition on the shape or size of recent moves. The open question is whether the flows themselves persist. The overnight drift shows that a flow effect can vanish when its source, here closing order imbalances, quietens, even without any arbitrage. So the useful output of the 6E fix and SOQ runs is not just a pass or fail. It is the time profile of each leg across 2018-2026, which shows whether the edge is fading the same way.

The second implication is that the cheapest improvement available may be sizing rather than signals. The strategies that already passed the holdout can be volatility-targeted and regime-gated with fewer than 100 cells each and no new alpha hypothesis. The published fight between Moreira-Muir and Cederburg is about Sharpe, not about tails, and every side agrees the tails shrink. If the overlay cuts drawdown on the noise-band and EMRT survivors at matched leverage, that alone can justify more capital in the existing book before any new family proves itself.
