# Month-End Treasury Flows Top a Thin Field

No published paper offers what the question asks for in full: an intraday, flat-by-the-close futures strategy with strong, independently replicated, net-of-cost evidence that holds after 2018. The literature has strong *gross, pre-2020* results and weak *post-2018, net* results, and the gap between them is exactly where the 6E FX-fix and ES drift-burst runs died. Within that limit, two families are worth a full funnel run. The first is the **Treasury month-end long** (Hartley and Schwarz), restricted to the day session of the last one to three trading days in ZN and ZB. It is the only candidate with an independent 2010–2026 futures test that still shows the effect (**ZN +5.2 bp per day, t = 4.1**), a flow mechanism that is growing rather than shrinking, and no overlap with any tested family. The second is **conditional last-half-hour momentum** (Baltussen, Da, Lammers and Martens, *JFE* 2021) on NQ, ES, YM, RTY and GC. It has the strongest paper of anything reviewed, but the unconditional version has been flat in the S&P 500 since about 2013, so only a version gated on signal size or volatility has a chance. It also shares the "trend into the close" bet with the noise-band book. Three event trades (the FOMC-day reversal in ES, EIA-day momentum in CL, and the Treasury auction-day V) are cheap to code but either trade too rarely for the funnel's trade-count gates or have gross edges below the user's cost. The honest prior for every candidate is that it fails the Sharpe ≥ 0.75 gate; the case for running the top two is that each is a small grid with a real mechanism, so a failure is informative and cheap.

## Only two families combine a mechanism with post-2018 life

The scoring below asks four questions of each candidate. Is the paper strong (venue, sample, full text read)? Is there any evidence the effect survived into 2018–2026? Does the gross edge per trade clear the user's round trip of 2 ticks plus $5 (ES $30, NQ/RTY/YM $15, ZN $36.25, ZB $67.50, CL and GC $25, 6E/6J $17.50 per contract)? And can the funnel test it at all, given its hard gates of **at least 100 completed development trades and at least 10 trades a year in 4 of the 5 development years** (stage 2) and **40 validation trades** (stage 8), as set in `drift-burst-backtest/configs/funnel/db_funnel_es_v2.yaml`?

The ranking rewards durability over paper prestige. Baltussen et al. is by far the strongest paper here, but its own S&P 500 effect has a near-zero slope in both of the independent post-2013 replications found, while the month-end effect has an out-of-sample futures test that runs through 2026. Every candidate below rank 2 has either too few events for the funnel's statistics or a published edge smaller than the user's cost.

## Month-end Treasury buying is the most durable edge

Hartley and Schwarz show that coupon Treasury excess returns are **positive and significant only in the last few days of the month**, and that a long held only over those days earns an **annualised Sharpe of about 1** over 1990–2018. They attribute it to window-dressing and index-extension demand from insurers and other benchmarked holders ([Hartley & Schwarz](https://www.kristaschwarz.com/EOM.pdf)). Their futures table is the key input. Held over the last *t* days, **10-year futures earn 9 bp of price at t = 1, 14 bp at t = 2, 17 bp at t = 3 and 22 bp at t = 5; 30-year futures earn 15, 21, 27 and 33 bp**. Windows of seven days or more are insignificant or negative for the 10-year ([Hartley & Schwarz, Table 8](https://www.kristaschwarz.com/EOM.pdf)). An independent practitioner test on futures from June 2010 to September 2026 finds the same shape: **ZN earns +5.2 bp per day over the last three trade dates (t = 4.1)**, the long bond +11.3 bp per day, and other days are slightly negative. The author warns that the three-day window was picked after looking at the chart ([OHLCV](https://ohlcv.io/posts/treasury-auction-cycle/)). That is a blog of unknown authorship, but its figures are detailed and internally consistent, and it is the only post-2018 evidence for any candidate in this report.

The mechanism is also getting stronger, not weaker. The Bloomberg index rebalancing time **moved from 3 p.m. to 4 p.m. ET on 14 January 2021**, and on month-end days the 4 p.m. window's share of daily Treasury volume rose from **11.6% in 2021 to 20.4% in 2025**, with about half of the 3:45–4:15 p.m. volume landing in the five minutes to 4:00 ([Liberty Street Economics](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/09/treasury-trading-at-the-close/)). That note reports volume only, not returns, so it shows where the flow goes, not that prices move with it. Contrast this with the overnight equity drift, whose source (closing order imbalances) shrank and took the effect with it.

The one unknown decides the outcome: **every published month-end number is close-to-close and includes the overnight session.** No source splits the effect between overnight and the day session. If most of the 15 bp on the long bond's last day accrues overnight or in Asian hours, the intraday version fails. The arithmetic shows how much has to be captured. At 36 trades a year (last three days), the Sharpe ≥ 0.75 gate needs net mean per trade of at least **0.125 times the per-trade standard deviation**; at 12 trades a year (last day only), 0.217 times. If ZN's 08:20–16:00 standard deviation is around 30 bp (my assumption; measure it), the three-day version needs about 3.75 bp net, or **about 7 bp gross per day**, which is more than the 5.2 bp close-to-close daily figure. ZB's last day is the most favourable cell: 15 bp gross against 5.9 bp of cost leaves about 9 bp net if it all accrues in session. The honest expectation is a standalone Sharpe of 0.3–0.7, with a pass only if the return concentrates around the strike. A failure here is still useful, because it is the first measurement of the intraday share.

**Rules.** On each of the last *k* trading days of the calendar month (k ∈ {1, 2, 3}, NYSE/CBOT calendar), buy ZN or ZB at the entry time and sell at the exit time; no other days trade. Before 14 January 2021 the index strike was 15:00 ET, afterwards 16:00 ET, so the exit should follow the regime. One engineering change is needed first: the shared session table in `fx-fix-backtest/src/data/futures.py` ends the ZN and ZB day session at 15:00, so the session must be extended to at least 16:05 for post-2021 exits. The Databento bars trade past that time, as the FX-fix project's 08:00–17:00 window showed. Placebos are part of the design: run the identical rule on the five trading days before the window and on randomly shifted day sets, and run "long every day, same clock" as the unconditional control.

| Axis | Values | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Contract | ZN, ZB | 2 |
| Days | last 1, last 2, last 3 (each day a separate trade) | 3 |
| Entry | 08:20, 10:00, 13:00 ET | 3 |
| Exit | strike-following (15:00 to Jan 2021, then 16:00); fixed 15:00 | 2 |
| Event filter | none; skip FOMC and coupon-auction days | 2 |

That is 72 cells, small enough that the expected maximum null Sharpe stays near 1.0–1.2 rather than the 1.7 of a 10,000-cell grid. Only the three-day cells clear stage 2 (about 180 development trades); one- and two-day cells give 60 and 120 trades and fail the 10-a-year floor. Either accept that only k = 3 can pass, or lower the floor for this family and record it as a new research trial. Treat ZN and ZB as one decision, since their daily returns are highly correlated.

## Last-half-hour momentum survives only when conditioned

Baltussen et al. show that the **rest-of-day return, from the prior close to 30 minutes before the close (rROD), predicts the last 30 minutes** across more than 60 futures from 1974 to 2020, with a reversal over the following days ([Baltussen et al.](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)). For equity-index futures as an equal-weight portfolio, the rule "long the last half hour if rROD > 0, else short" earned **6.86% a year gross at 3.96% volatility, a Sharpe of 1.73 and a 55% hit rate**; requiring rROD and the first-half-hour signal to agree raised the hit rate to 61% at a Sharpe of 1.60. The authors do not model costs beyond one sentence: S&P 500 futures keep a **positive net Sharpe at a one-tick cost** ([Baltussen et al.](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)). The commodity basket earned 4.34% gross (Sharpe 1.42), and **GC is the only commodity with a significant out-of-sample R² (0.08–0.18%)**. CL is insignificant with negative out-of-sample R², and 6E shows nothing ([Baltussen et al., Appendix B](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)). The proposed mechanism is dealers and leveraged ETFs hedging short gamma in the direction of the day's move, and the effect is much stronger on days of negative dealer gamma ([Baltussen et al.](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf); [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/hot-topic-does-gamma-hedging-actually-affect-stock-prices/)).

The decay evidence is just as specific. On SPY 1-minute bars, the Gao-style slope is **0.091 (t 2.64) in 2008–2012 and 0.003 (t 0.20) in 2013–2019**, zero or negative in every year from 2015 to 2019 ([Temperance Capital](https://temperancecapital.substack.com/p/market-intraday-momentum-replicates)). On 1,085 SPX sessions from 2022 to 2026, the rROD slope is **+0.006 (t 0.6), "flat in every single year"**, but on the roughly 15% of days when dealers were short gamma on the vendor's own tape-signed book it is **+0.055 (t 3.1), positive in all five years**. A public open-interest gamma proxy showed no effect, and the vendor calls the result "not worth a position" ([FirmTape](https://dev.to/firmtape/intraday-momentum-is-dead-in-the-0dte-era-we-measured-it-on-1085-spx-sessions-43g0)). Rosa's peer-reviewed test finds the first-half-hour version disappears out of sample, but a thresholded, signal-strength version beats the always-on one ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v42y2022i12p2218-2234.html)), and Li, Sakkas and Urquhart find intraday momentum stronger when volatility is high and liquidity low ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v57y2022ics138641812100001x.html)). Both of those are blog or vendor sources on the decay side, and none tests futures after May 2020. The user's 2018–2026 sample sits entirely inside the decayed window, which makes this run a direct test of whether any bar-observable condition recovers the short-gamma days.

The overlap with the book needs saying plainly. The earlier survey rejected this family as the same bet as noise-band breakout, and the risk is real: on a breakout day the noise-band strategy is usually already long or short in the day's direction at 15:30, so it already holds most of this exposure. What is new is a fixed-time entry on *every* day, including days without a breakout, and conditioning on size or volatility. The test that matters after the funnel is the correlation of daily P&L with the noise-band survivors, not standalone Sharpe.

**Edge versus cost.** The equity figure of 6.86% a year over about 252 trades is **about 2.7 bp gross per trade**, a 1974–2020 average before the decay. In dollars that is $36 per ES contract at 2018 prices against $30 of cost, and $81 at 2025 prices. NQ is the cheapest venue by far: the same 2.7 bp is about $38 against $15 in 2018 and $113 in 2025. By my arithmetic from CME tick sizes, costs in basis points roughly halved between the development and holdout periods as index levels rose (ES from about 2.2 bp to 1.0 bp, NQ from 1.1 bp to 0.4 bp), so a cell that is marginal in 2018–2022 can look better later for cost reasons alone. For GC, an approximate per-contract estimate (ρ ≈ 0.05 from the published R², σ ≈ 0.15% for the last half hour) gives about **0.6 bp, or $24 against $25 of cost** at a $400k notional, and about a third of that in 2018 dollars. GC therefore works only with a filter that concentrates trades on large-signal days. A 2026 walk-forward study of 14 OHLCV intraday momentum families on MNQ found **none** survived, mostly because gross edges of 0.07–1.5 points sat below a 2-point friction floor ([arXiv 2605.04004](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04004)), which is the base rate this run should expect.

**Rules.** At 15:30 ET (GC: 13:00 ET; the project's GC session already ends at the 13:30 settlement, matching the paper), compute rROD from the prior day-session close. Go long if rROD > 0 and the filter is on, short if rROD < 0, and exit at the session close. Use only bars that open before the close; a public replication's apparent late reversal turned out to be a bug from including the 16:00 post-close bar ([market-anomaly-replications](https://github.com/talalmalikllc-debug/market-anomaly-replications)).

| Axis | Values | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Signal | rROD from prior close; from today's open (no gap); first half hour incl. gap (Gao) | 3 |
| Window | enter T−30, exit T; enter T−30, exit T−10; enter T−10, exit T | 3 |
| Filter | none; \|rROD\| > 0.5σ₂₀; \|rROD\| > 1σ₂₀; 20-day RV above 1-year median; rROD < 0 only (put-heavy proxy) | 5 |
| Sign agreement with first half hour | off, on | 2 |

That is 90 cells per contract. Run NQ as the primary, ES, YM and RTY with unchanged rules as transfer checks, and GC as a separate line. The last-10-minute window comes from a thesis reporting that most of the remaining predictability sits there ([Aalto](https://aaltodoc.aalto.fi/items/1011f043-edd1-4cb2-9694-b1216d77561f)). Filtered cells trade 50–120 times a year, enough for both trade-count gates.

## Event-day trades are cheap to code but too rare for the funnel

**FOMC-day reversal.** On scheduled FOMC days, at 13:50 ET, buy ES if its prior 24-hour return is negative and sell if positive, then exit at the end of the session. Over October 1997 to January 2020 and 180 announcements, this earned **+0.18% net per announcement filled at bid and ask, with a Sharpe of 0.36** on the authors' per-day definition ([CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/calendar-effects/stock-market-return-reversal-after-fomc-announcements); [Papers With Backtest](https://paperswithbacktest.com/strategies/the-fomc-announcement-reversal)). At 18 bp, the edge is roughly ten times the ES round trip, the best edge-to-cost ratio here. It is not the pre-FOMC drift, which held positions overnight and has largely vanished since 2015 ([Kurov, Wolfe & Gilbert](https://www.skidmore.edu/economics/documents/KurovWolfeGilbert-TheDisappearingPre-FOMC-Announce-Drift-200914.pdf)). The problem is power. There are about 40 meetings in development and 16 in validation, so the strategy fails stage 2 and stage 8 by construction, and a published Sharpe of 0.36 is already below the 0.75 bar. Run it as a pre-registered side test on ES, NQ, YM and RTY with at most 12 cells (signal window {24 h, RTH open to entry}, exit {15:00, 16:00}, threshold {0, 0.25%, 0.5%}), and judge it on a bootstrap interval for mean return per event and a randomly-shifted-date placebo over 2018–2024.

**CL EIA-day momentum.** Wen, Indriawan, Lien and Xu find that on Wednesday EIA days the **third half-hour return predicts the last half hour**, while on other days only the first half hour does, mostly through its overnight component ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/aen/journl/ej44-5-xu.html)). Search summaries quote Sharpe ratios of 9–12 that could not be verified in the paper and are almost certainly gross ([Adelaide repository](https://digital.library.adelaide.edu.au/dspace/bitstream/2440/141224/2/hdl_141224.pdf)). The result also conflicts with Baltussen's insignificant CL coefficients over 1987–2020 ([Baltussen et al.](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)). A sibling idea from Geman and Li, following the 10:20–10:25 move into the 10:30 release, was "remarkably profitable" in their sample but rests on early access to the data, a leak that tighter release procedures were meant to close ([Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/journal-of-energy-markets/5728636/an-analysis-of-intraday-market-response-to-crude-oil-inventory-shocks)). CL's round trip is about 3.6 bp, so the edge has to be several times the commodity-basket average of 1.7 bp. With about 50 EIA days a year the funnel can test it. Use the CL 09:00–14:30 session. Signal ∈ {10:00–10:30, 10:30–11:00, 09:00–10:30, release to 14:00}, trade window ∈ {14:00–14:30, 13:30–14:30}, filter ∈ {none, |signal| > 1σ₂₀}, and day set ∈ {EIA days, non-EIA days as control} gives 32 cells, plus 6 cells for the pre-release follow. Holiday weeks shift the release, so take timestamps from the EIA calendar.

**Treasury auction-day V.** Yields rise in the hours before coupon auctions and fall after, driven by dealer order flow, and the pressure has not grown in recent years ([NY Fed SR1188](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/staff_reports/sr1188.html)). The 2010–2026 futures test (1,250 auctions) finds a ZN swing of **+4.0 bp against controls (t 2.4)**, but only **+2.6 bp gross per auction across two legs and +0.1 bp net at one tick per round trip**. Most of the daily gap disappears once month-end days are removed, because every month ends within a week of a 2-, 5- and 7-year auction ([OHLCV](https://ohlcv.io/posts/treasury-auction-cycle/)). At the user's two-tick round trip it loses money in ZN. Only the long leg in ZB on 20- and 30-year auctions (about 24 a year) is worth a footnote run, with post-window ∈ {60, 180 min, to 16:00} and a pre-auction drawdown condition ∈ {none, ≤ −5 bp}. Because of the month-end confound, it must exclude month-end days, or it will rediscover candidate 1.

## The fix and burst failures predict which papers will die

The two most recent runs show the failure mode clearly. The **FX-fix reversal on 6E had 0 of 405 configurations profitable, a best PF of 0.96, and gross edges of $16–56 per trade against about $140 of costs**. The paper's net results were computed at half of an indicative spot spread, and its Tokyo leg had already decayed after 2013 ([Bank of Canada SWP 2021-48](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/swp2021-48.pdf)). The lesson is that the dollar edge per contract must be computed against the actual round trip *before* any code is written; for the 6E fix that check would have shown a 3–9× shortfall. The **drift-burst fade on ES passed nothing: v1 0 of 576, v2 0 of 10,368, best Sharpe 0.47 with PF 1.42 on only 63 trades**. Its paper reported no trading P&L, no costs and no out-of-sample test, measured returns on mid-quotes, and ended in 2017 ([arXiv 2601.08974](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08974)). The lesson there is that enlarging the grid 18× did not find an edge the paper never claimed, and only raised the deflated-Sharpe hurdle. Using the deflated-Sharpe framework with independent trials, my estimate of the expected maximum null Sharpe over a five-year development window is about **1.0 at 50 trials, 1.4 at 500 and 1.7 at 10,000** ([Bailey & López de Prado](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551)), so a 10,000-cell grid needs a true Sharpe well above the 0.75 gate just to be distinguishable from luck.

Three screening rules follow. First, a candidate qualifies only if the paper reports a per-trade return, or enough to derive one, and that gross figure is at least about 2× the user's round trip at 2018 prices. Second, grids stay under about 100 cells per contract and every axis is anchored to a published parameter. Third, every clock or calendar rule carries a placebo (shifted dates or a negative-control contract). The broader literature supports this caution. Published anomalies lose **26% of their return out of sample and 58% after publication** ([McLean & Pontiff](https://ivey.uwo.ca/media/3775549/pontiff.pdf)); **65% of 452 anomalies fail plain replication, including 96% of the trading-frictions category** ([Hou, Xue & Zhang](https://nber.org/papers/w23394)); and new findings should clear **t ≈ 3** ([Harvey, Liu & Zhu](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592)). The only post-2018 durability evidence for the noise-band strategy already in the book is also negative: an exact reproduction looked very different on May 2024–March 2026 data ([Swedroe](https://larryswedroe.substack.com/p/when-beat-the-market-meets-the-real)).

The rejected ideas fail those rules for specific reasons.

Two of the earlier survey's leads also fall to the no-overnight rule. The 3rd-Friday SOQ "tent" earns its rise from Thursday's close to Friday's opening quotation ([AEA 2025](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2025/program/paper/N7rsBN2N)), so only its Friday post-SOQ reversal leg (09:30–16:00) is admissible. The overnight drift is overnight by definition and averaged about zero over 2021–2025 ([Liberty Street 2026](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/07/the-disappearing-overnight-drift/)).

## Conclusion

The pattern across both surveys is that a durable intraday edge needs a buyer who is *forced* to trade at a known time *and* whose demand is not shrinking. The FX fix had the first but not the second after 2013. The overnight drift lost its source. Last-half-hour momentum's hedgers moved to 0DTE books whose gamma sign the bars cannot see. Month-end index extension is the one flow in this set that a public source shows growing in the user's sample, which is why it ranks first despite a weaker paper. The question it can answer, whether the effect lives in the day session or overnight, is not answered anywhere in the literature, so even a failed run produces a new fact.

The deeper implication is that the funnel's gates now decide which papers can be tested at all. Stage 2's 100-trade, 10-per-year floor rules out every event strategy with fewer than about 20 events a year, which is where the largest per-trade edges here sit (FOMC +18 bp net, ZB month-end ≈ 15 bp gross). Strategies that trade often enough to pass that gate have per-trade edges near the cost. A second, event-study track, with pre-registered single configurations, bootstrap intervals and shifted-date placebos, would let the book test the high-edge, low-frequency ideas honestly instead of filtering them out by design.
