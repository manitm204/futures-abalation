2023–2024 is not clean for this round. The grid was chosen after seeing round 1 and the 15-minute round on 2023–2024, so passing validation here is weak evidence. The 2025+ holdout is untouched.

Most versions still lose on 2018–2022. About 40% made money, and 2,625 cleared the profit gate (Sharpe ≥ 0.75). That is more than 2,000, so the prescreen kept the best 3 of each of 110 groups (326 versions). The cap was lowered from 3,000 to 2,000 before any stage-4 result of this run was read. What helped:

  • Short squeezes: length 3 was 59% profitable, falling to 17% at length 80.
  • The EMA(50) trend filter mattered most: 49% profitable vs 31% without, and 2,440 of the 2,625 that cleared the profit gate used it.
  • A wide Keltner channel (2.5 ATR, 57%) over a very tight one (0.5 ATR, 13%).
  • A 2- or 3-ATR stop (49%) over a 1-ATR stop (21%). Target, setup expiry, minimum box width and the minimum squeeze bars made little difference.

153 finalists from 77 groups.

90 of 153 passed 2023–2024, from 46 groups. The median passer made +6.1% over two years at Sharpe 0.68, profit factor 1.15, drawdown −4.3%. Across all 153 the median was +3.3%, Sharpe 0.40, profit factor 1.07. 22 finalists had a 2023–2024 Sharpe of 1.0 or more.

  • Holding ES made +41% (Sharpe 1.40, drawdown −11%). The best finalists earned about half of that, at under a third of the drawdown.
  • Long squeezes fell apart: 0 of 20 finalists at length 60 or 80 passed, against 31 of 35 at length 8.
  • Long-and-short passed slightly more often (49 of 80) than long-only (41 of 73), but every one of the top versions is long-only with the EMA filter.
  • The 2018–2022 ranking said little about 2023–2024 (rank correlation 0.20).

The best versions clearly beat random entries. The median finalist beat 85% of random-entry runs on Sharpe; 34 beat 95% and 19 beat 99%. Random long-only trades did better than random two-way trades in 2023–2024 (median Sharpe −0.05 vs −0.44).
