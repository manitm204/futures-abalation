What changed from v4. Only the parameter grid. Average length is now 20–80 (6 values) and volatility length 10–80 (8 values), so the region the earlier survivors used is tested densely. Entry z is −2/−2.5/−3, max hold 5/10 and the stop none/1/2 × ATR. That makes 41,472 versions. The funnel is identical to v4, including the local-nudge stability test (≥ 70% profitable, median Sharpe ≥ 0.5).

Bar size still dominates. On 2018–2022, 45% of 1-hour versions made money (median Sharpe 0.00), against 14% on 15-minute and 3% on 5-minute bars. No stop beat 1 × ATR. Volatility length 20 was the strongest value, and average length mattered little.

Validation now needs 40 trades, down from 60. The trade minimum was lowered after the first v5 validation, which passed 2 of 45; with 60 trades, several 1-hour versions that made money were cut only for trading too rarely. Everything else about validation is unchanged (net > 0, Sharpe ≥ 0.30, drawdown no worse than 30%).

10 of 45 finalists now pass 2023–2024, from 7 different groups. The strongest are #3 and #4 (same group: 15-minute SMA 40, volatility 20, entry −3, hold 5, up-bar confirmation, no stop). They scored Sharpe 0.82 and 0.92 (+3.8% and +4.0%, 65–66 trades), and 1.00 and 1.09 on NQ over 2018–2022. Next come three 1-hour versions: #37 with Sharpe 1.01 (+6.0%, 42 trades), #25 with 0.76 (+5.9%, 43 trades) and #19 with 0.41 (48 trades). These also did well on NQ (0.59–0.95). Several passers scraped by: #21 at a Sharpe of exactly 0.30, #27 at 0.32, and #38, #40 and #45, whose NQ Sharpe was about zero or negative.

Fewer trades means noisier verdicts. At 40–48 trades over two years, one or two trades can flip a pass to a fail. Six of the ten passers are 1-hour versions in that range.

The finer grid filled the average-length gap: it's a plateau. For the passing setup at volatility 20, average length 30, 40, 50 and 60 give Sharpe 0.70, 0.91, 0.80 and 0.74.

Volatility length is a knife edge. At average length 40, volatility length 10, 15, 20, 25 and 30 gives Sharpe −0.53, −0.23, 0.91, 0.55 and 0.27, and the nudges to 16 and 24 give −0.56 and −0.17. A real effect should not flip sign between 16 and 20. This looks like 20 happening to line up with the 2018–2022 data, and it's the main reason not to trust the pass.

Rarely in the market, small returns. The passers held a position about 3% of the time and made about +4% over 2023–2024, against +40.7% for holding ES.

Not a clean test. This grid was designed after reading v3 and v4's 2023–2024 results, so validation on ES is now partly in-sample for this strategy. That is recorded in the run's config. The 2025+ holdout is still untouched.
