Mostly a losing idea on ES during regular hours. Only 16% of the 46,080 versions made money on 2018–2022, and the median Sharpe was −0.45. Bar size mattered most: 29% of 1-hour versions were profitable, against 14% on 15-minute and 5% on 5-minute bars. Deeper entries (z −2.5 or −3) did better than −1.5. Running with no stop beat every ATR stop. Average length, exit level and the up-bar confirmation barely mattered.

The 1-hour finalists fell apart on new data. Nine of the eleven finalists were on 1-hour bars. The six with entry z −2 and volatility length 80 all lost money on 2023–2024 and were also weak on NQ. The two 1-hour versions at z −3 stayed profitable (Sharpe 0.70 and 0.51), but traded too rarely (37 and 42 trades) to meet the 60-trade mark.

The one survivor passed by the narrowest margin. #4 (15-minute bars, EMA 40, volatility 20, entry −2.5, exit −0.5, hold 16, up-bar confirmation, no stop) made a Sharpe of 0.99 on 2023–2024 with exactly 60 trades. Its near-twin #8 scored 1.01 but made 59 trades, so it failed. On NQ over 2018–2022 the survivor managed only a Sharpe of 0.30.

Small returns, because it is rarely in the market. The finalists held a position only 5–9% of the time. The survivor made +5.0% over 2023–2024, while simply holding ES (rolled, unlevered) made +40.7%. Its drawdown was much smaller (−2.3% against −11.3%), so any value is as a low-exposure add-on, not a replacement for holding the index.

One pass out of eleven, from a 46,080-version search, is weak evidence. The 2025+ holdout has not been run.
