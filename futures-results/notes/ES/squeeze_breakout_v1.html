ES squeeze round 1. 20,736 versions on 5- and 15-minute bars, fixed before any compression-breakout result was seen:

  • Squeeze = Bollinger Bands (2 sd) inside a Keltner Channel: length 10/20/30 bars, Keltner width 1.0/1.5/2.0 ATR, at least 1 or 4 squeeze bars in a row.
  • Entry on a close outside the box (next open) or a stop at the box edge (wick); the box stays live 3 or 6 bars.
  • Stop at the far side of the box or 1/2 ATR; target 1R/2R/3R/none; long, short or both; EMA(50) trend filter on/off; minimum box width any/0.5 ATR.

ES 2023–2024 had not been used to score any compression-breakout version before this run. It had been used for the mean-reversion and noise-band families.

Most versions lose on 2018–2022. Only 33% made money (median Sharpe −0.19), and 254 of 20,736 cleared the profit gate (Sharpe ≥ 0.75). What helped: 15-minute bars over 5 (42% vs 24% profitable), wick entries over closes (39% vs 27%), the EMA(50) filter (37% vs 28%), and wider or no targets (1R 22% profitable, none 42%). A 1-ATR stop was worst. The minimum box width barely mattered. 63 finalists from 33 groups.

The winning "squeeze" is hardly a squeeze. 47 of 63 finalists, and all 34 that passed with a Keltner width of 2.0, use the widest channel. On ES 15-minute bars, length 10 with width 2.0 flags 77% of all bars as a squeeze (length 30: 25%). So these versions mostly trade breakouts of the last ~10 bars' range, with a trend filter. Genuinely tight settings (width 1.0, firing on 1–25% of bars) made 14 finalists, and all 14 failed 2023–2024.

36 of 63 passed 2023–2024, from 20 groups. The median passer made +6.2% over two years at Sharpe 0.73, profit factor 1.17, drawdown −2.9%, in the market 16% of the time. Across all 63 finalists the median Sharpe was 0.33.

  • Holding ES made +41% (Sharpe 1.40, drawdown −11%). Every finalist earned far less than that, at a much smaller drawdown.
  • Long-only held up best: all 20 long-only finalists passed, 13 of 32 long-and-short, and only 3 of 11 short-only. The two worst results were short-only (−13%).
  • The 2018–2022 ranking carried over weakly (rank correlation 0.29). How a version did on NQ 2018–2022 with the same rules pointed the wrong way for 2023–2024 (−0.21).

Some beat luck, but not by much. The median finalist beat 80% of random-entry runs on Sharpe; 19 beat 95% (11 groups) and 4 beat 99%. Random intraday trades lost money in 2023–2024 in almost every group (median Sharpe −0.13 for 15-minute long-only), so beating them is a low bar.

  • #5 / #6 (15 min, length 10, width 2.0, wick, box stop, 3R target, long, no trend filter): +9.9%, Sharpe 1.05, profit factor 1.21, beat 96% of random runs.
  • #23 / #24 (5 min, length 10, width 2.0, 4 squeeze bars, wick, 2-ATR stop, no target, long, EMA filter): the best 2023–2024 result, +12.8%, Sharpe 1.43, profit factor 1.34, beat 99%.
  • #9 / #10 (5 min, length 10, width 2.0, 4 bars, wick, box stop, 3R, both ways, EMA filter, 0.5-ATR box): +10.2%, Sharpe 0.93, beat 97%, the best of the long-and-short versions.

Search-adjusted, nothing is significant. The highest deflated Sharpe is 0.47 (#1), well below the 0.95 bar, after about 1,166 effective trials. Many finalists come in near-identical pairs (setup live 3 vs 6 bars, box width any vs 0.5 ATR), so there are fewer distinct ideas here than the count suggests. The 2025+ holdout is untouched.
