Same rules as 6E v5, plus a cross-market check on the yen (6J), now built. 6E 2023–2024 is partly in-sample here, since the v5 results shaped the grid.

On 2018–2022 the grid is a coin flip. 50% of versions made money, and the median Sharpe was 0.02. That's better than v5's 4.9%, because the grid now sits where v5 found its only survivors.

  • SMA beat EMA (median Sharpe 0.22 against −0.22). No stop beat any stop (0.22 against −0.12 to 0.03).
  • Shallow entries did better (−1.5: 0.17; −2.5: −0.19). Holds of 4–5 bars did better than 8 (0.15–0.19 against −0.28). Volatility 15 was weak (−0.28).
  • 1,053 versions cleared the profit gate, 1,018 passed the stable-settings test, and dedup left 252 finalists in 135 groups.

0 of 252 finalists passed 2023–2024. Only 4 made money, and the median finalist lost 3.2%, Sharpe −0.92, profit factor 0.66, on 69 trades. Every one failed on Sharpe, so the trade count wasn't the problem.

  • The top 4 on 2018–2022 are the v5 setting with a 4-bar hold (SMA 80/10, entry −2 to −2.25). They made +12–14% (Sharpe 1.3–1.4, PF 1.7–1.9), then lost about 1% on 2023–2024 (Sharpe −0.2 to −0.4, PF 0.83–0.90).
  • The best 2023–2024 result, #146 (SMA 80/8, entry −1.5, exit −0.5, hold 5), made +0.5%, Sharpe 0.17, PF 1.08.

The yen didn't back it up. Run unchanged on 6J over 2018–2022, the finalists' median Sharpe was −0.15, and only 68 of 252 were positive.

Not better than luck. The median finalist beat 17% of random-entry runs on Sharpe, and none beat 90%. Holding the euro lost 6.6% over 2023–2024.
